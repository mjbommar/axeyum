ADR-2132: DOES THIS LANE'S A/B REPRODUCE ADR-2125's?
=====================================================

An A/B is only worth reading if the harness under it is stable. ADR-2125 ran the
same two arms (`off` and `on`) on the same corpus at the same 24 s / 8 GiB
envelope on the same two core pairs of the same host -- on a different day, on a
different binary, from a different branch base. Its per-file verdicts are
committed at `../lra-warm-basis-20260916/ab.QF_LRA*.tsv`.

So the two runs can be compared where they overlap, and that comparison is a
control on the MEASUREMENT rather than on the lever.

    ADR-2125 rows           195
    this lane (partial)     150
    overlap                 120

    `off` arm agrees on     119 / 120
    `on`  arm agrees on     119 / 120
    rows where either differs                     1

THE ONE DIFFERENCE MOVES BOTH ARMS TOGETHER
-------------------------------------------

    clock_synchro/clocksynchro_7clocks.worst_case_skew.induct
        ADR-2125   off=unknown   on=unknown
        this lane  off=unsat     on=unsat

Both arms moved, and they moved the same way. So it is not attributable to the
lever in either run: it is either the ambient 1-1.5 % flip rate these boxes
carry at a 24 s budget on a file sitting near the boundary, or a change on `main`
between the two branch bases (ADR-2125 branched at `5cb9553a6`; this lane merged
`677c0192c`, which carries the NRA-CELL-EXACT merge among others). This lane does
not say which, because it did not measure it -- and it does not need to, since a
row that moves in BOTH arms cancels in the difference the A/B reports.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT LICENSE
-----------------------------------

It licenses reading the two lanes' numbers as one series: 119/120 across two
runs is the harness, the envelope, the core pinning and the corpus all holding
still. It is the control that makes "ADR-2125 measured -9.2 %, this lane
measures X" a comparison rather than two anecdotes.

It does NOT license carrying ADR-2125's mover classifications forward. A stable
loss is a claim about a 3x-per-arm recheck on a specific binary, and this lane
re-checks its own movers rather than inheriting them -- which is also why
section 2's re-sizing of `uart-8.induction.cvc` was worth taking: ADR-2125's
reading of that row was explicitly a hypothesis, and 119/120 agreement on
verdicts says nothing about whether a hypothesis about BUILDS was right.
