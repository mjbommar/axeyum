## THE SHIP CRITERION IS NOT MET

0 stable losses was the condition. There is **1 STABLE-LOSS**. The lever ships
OFF and ADR-2120 stays `proposed`. This is the whole decision and it does not
turn on anything else in the lane.

## WHAT THE RE-CHECK CORRECTED, IN BOTH DIRECTIONS

**A raw loss evaporated.** `NB19-026` was one of the A/B's two losses. Across six
re-check passes NEITHER arm decides it: the A/B's arm A had decided it once, at
1.5 s, and that single decision was the whole "loss". Reporting the raw column
would have charged the lever for a file it does not affect.

**And one of THIS LANE'S OWN CLAIMS was refuted.** `REACH-ENGINE.txt` recorded
`LabelStmt.011` converting to `unsat` on the ON arm and drew a conclusion from
it -- "the conversion is REAL and it is MARGINAL AGAINST THE CLOCK". Three passes
per arm return `unknown` 3 of 3 on arm B. The single `unsat` does not reproduce.
That paragraph is retracted in place rather than deleted, with this measurement
beside it.

The rule it re-earns is one this repository already has, and it applies to a
lane's own output exactly as it applies to a handoff it inherits: *a traced
plan's "verified numerically" is itself a claim -- re-run its numbers, do not
inherit them.* One observation of a verdict at the edge of a 24 s budget is not
a measurement, whoever made it.

## WHAT SURVIVES THE CORRECTION

The COUNTERS, not the verdicts. `rej_handoff` moving 0 -> 18,688 and
`rej_nocontext` 33,090 -> 0 on a single run is not a coin flip between two runs;
a verdict at the budget edge is, and this file proved it. So the supported claim
is that the mechanism executes at scale on real files and empties the bucket
ADR-2113 located -- and NOT that it converts them.
