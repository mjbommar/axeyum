# cvc5 — inventory and gaps (2026-09-09)

**Scope.** `references/cvc5/`, commit `1689f13331f7543801f82d9dcbcaac2f70a26781`
("Fix nightlies (#12909)", 2026-09-03), shallow clone, 102 MB on disk.
517,332 lines across 1,659 `.cpp`/`.h` files under `src/`, plus 12,530 lines of
Eunoia (`.eo`) proof signatures under `proofs/` and 4,184 `.smt2` regression
benchmarks under `test/regress/cli/`. C++17, modified BSD (`COPYING:1-20`);
optional GPL leaf dependencies (CLN, glpk-cut-log, CoCoALib, Normaliz) are
off by default.

Two adjacent clones are cited where they settle a question about cvc5's output:
`references/carcara` (`6624ea80cf1985ada473c0705869c78353e4282d`, 2026-08-12),
the Alethe checker cvc5's own regression suite shells out to and the one our
`CARCARA_CHECKED_RULES` list is pinned against. **`references/` contains no
`ethos` clone**, so every claim below about Ethos/Eunoia is read off cvc5's own
`proofs/eo/` signatures and `test/regress/cli/run_regression.py`, never off the
checker itself.

Everything here is a source read. Nothing was built, configured, or run.

## Summary

- **cvc5's proof system is a first-class internal calculus, not a printer.**
  **172** `ProofRule` enumerators and **534** `ProofRewriteRule` enumerators in
  one public header (`include/cvc5/cvc5_proof_rule.h:89`, `:2760`). Four of the
  172 are bookkeeping (`UNKNOWN`, `LAST`, `LFSC_RULE`, `ALETHE_RULE`), so ~168
  are real rules; **143** have an in-tree checker registered through
  `ProofChecker::registerChecker` (`src/proof/proof_checker.cpp:239`), spread
  over 10 per-theory `proof_checker.cpp` files. The 534 rewrite rules are
  `NONE` + 93 hand-written + 439 auto-generated from RARE + `LAST`
  (the 439 confirmed by counting the
  `/** Auto-generated from RARE rule … */` tags). Four external formats are
  printed *from* that calculus — CPC/Eunoia (default), Alethe, LFSC, DOT.
- **The default output format is CPC, not Alethe.** `proof-format-mode`
  defaults to `CPC` (`src/options/proof_options.toml:9`), checked by the
  external Ethos checker against the 51 `.eo` signature files in `proofs/eo/`.
  Alethe is a secondary, explicitly-experimental target.
- **cvc5 has our "rules the checker accepts that Carcara does not" problem, and
  it is much larger than ours.** Its Alethe post-processor translates 97 of 172
  `ProofRule`s (`src/proof/alethe/alethe_post_processor.cpp`); the other 75 fall
  through a `default:` arm to `AletheRule::HOLE` (`:3129-3143`). The untranslated
  set is the **entire** strings family (20 rules), the finite-field family (8),
  sets (4), `DT_SPLIT`, `SAT_REFUTATION`/`DRAT_REFUTATION`, and the 22
  `ARITH_TRANS_*` transcendental rules. Nineteen of those string rules *do* have
  an in-tree cvc5 checker (`src/theory/strings/proof_checker.cpp`) — they simply
  never reach Alethe.
- **Their Alethe bit-blasting vocabulary does not match Carcara's.** cvc5 emits
  `bv_bitblast_step_bvadd`, `…_bvand`, … (`src/proof/alethe/alethe_proof_rule.cpp:144-166`);
  Carcara's dispatch table has `bitblast_add`, `bitblast_and`, …
  (`carcara/src/checker/shared.rs:332`), and `grep -rn "bv_bitblast_step" carcara/`
  returns **0**. Eighteen bit-blast rule names, plus 12 more, are in cvc5's
  Alethe output and absent from Carcara's checker: 30 names total.
- **cvc5's Alethe gate runs Carcara for real and can fail — but with a
  three-name allowlist.** `test/regress/cli/run_regression.py:265-341` invokes
  `carcara check --allowed-rules undefined la_mult_sign la_mult_abs_comparison`
  on every `unsat` regression, and fails the test unless the output says
  `valid`. `undefined` is the hole marker under `--proof-alethe-testing`, so the
  gate tolerates arbitrary holes by construction. It also `EXIT_SKIP`s when cvc5
  prints `Proof unsupported by Alethe`. This is the same shape as our
  `portable_artifact` vocabulary gate, made executable.
- **Strings is where the capability gap is widest.** `src/theory/strings/` is
  36,752 lines over 80 files, driving a 20-step `InferStep` strategy
  (`src/theory/strings/strategy.h:33-79`, ordered at `strategy.cpp`), 87 distinct
  `STRINGS_*` inference ids (`src/theory/inference_id.h`), 50 string/sequence
  term kinds (`src/theory/strings/kinds.toml`), and 159 RARE rewrite rules for
  strings alone (`src/theory/strings/rewrites`).
- **cvc5 reduces extended string functions to bounded-quantified arithmetic;
  we reduce them to bounded bit-vectors.** `TheoryStringsPreprocess::reduce`
  (`src/theory/strings/theory_strings_preprocess.cpp:57-1000`) emits reduction
  lemmas for 15 operators, which is why enabling strings force-enables
  `BoundedIntegers` and `ModelEngine`
  (`src/theory/quantifiers/quantifiers_modules.cpp`, the `|| options.strings.stringExp`
  conditions). This is an architectural difference, not a feature gap — see
  *Not comparable*.
- **Quantifiers is the largest subsystem in the tree**: 93,287 lines over 299
  files, 12 pluggable modules registered in
  `src/theory/quantifiers/quantifiers_modules.cpp`. Conflict-based
  instantiation, E-matching, and pool instantiation are on by default; CEGQI is
  turned on by logic for any quantified arithmetic/BV/datatype/FP problem
  (`src/smt/set_defaults.cpp:1627-1643`).
- **cvc5 has no optimization/OMT layer at all** (`src/omt/` does not exist;
  `grep -rn OptimizationSolver references/cvc5/` returns zero hits). We do
  (`optimize.rs` 1,392 lines, `maxsat.rs`, 11 `Solver<B>` methods —
  `docs/solver-inventory-2026-09/05-bitvector-arrays-fp-and-datatypes.md:259`).
- **cvc5's interpolation and abduction are SyGuS synthesis queries to a
  subsolver, checked a posteriori** (`src/smt/interpolation_solver.cpp`,
  `src/smt/abduction_solver.cpp`). Ours are nine proof-based interpolators
  (Farkas, McMillan-off-LRAT, Ackermann-then-LRA) each with a
  `verify_interpolant` (`docs/solver-inventory-2026-09/08-models-proofs-and-evidence.md:436-444`).
- **The SAT layer is delegated.** CaDiCaL is the default for both the CDCL(T)
  solver and bit-blasting (`src/options/prop_options.toml:5-15`,
  `src/options/bv_options.toml:5-18`); cvc5 sets five CaDiCaL options total and
  owns no restart policy, no clause-DB reduction, and no phase-saving of its
  own. Theory propagation goes through CaDiCaL's IPASIR-UP external-propagator
  interface (`src/prop/cadical/cdclt_propagator.h:23-25`).
- **cvc5's own regression suite for strings is 583 files; we vendored 20 of
  them.** See *The vendored corpus slice* below.

### The three biggest things cvc5 does that we do not

1. **A full string decision procedure with proof-carrying inferences.**
   87 named inference rules, normal-form computation with loop detection and
   disequality extensionality, regex unfolding plus derivatives plus an NFA
   evaluator, and 19 in-tree-checked string proof rules. Our word core has four
   inference rules and one split family
   (`docs/solver-inventory-2026-09/07-strings-and-regex.md`).
2. **Six instantiation strategies we have no analogue for** — conflict-based
   (QCF), CEGQI with per-theory instantiators, enumerative/full-saturate,
   pool-based, SyGuS-based, and sub-conflict — plus a trie-based instantiation
   dedup and an instantiation evaluator (`ieval/`) that filters candidate
   instantiations before they become lemmas.
3. **A DSL rewrite-rule database (RARE) whose *only* job is proof
   reconstruction.** 439 rules across 13 extension-less `rewrites*` files
   (there is no `.rare` file extension in the tree; the files are listed in
   `src/CMakeLists.txt:1467-1484`), code-generated by
   `src/rewriter/mkrewrites.py` into a `RewriteDb` and into 439 auto-generated
   `ProofRewriteRule` enumerators. The `RewriteDb` is built **only** when
   `--proof-granularity` is `dsl-rewrite` or `dsl-rewrite-strict`
   (`src/smt/proof_manager.cpp:52-58`), and its single consumer is
   `RewriteDbProofCons`, which replaces premise-less `TRUST`/`TRUST_THEORY_REWRITE`
   steps with checked `DSL_REWRITE` derivations
   (`src/smt/proof_post_processor_dsl.cpp:68-84`). Nothing in
   `src/theory/rewriter.cpp` or any `*_rewriter.cpp` touches it — **the rewriter
   itself never consults the rule database**. That is the design idea worth
   stealing: a machine-readable spelling of the rewrites, used to turn trusted
   steps into checked ones after the fact. Our rewrite manifest has 59 shipped
   rules and no proof-reconstruction role
   (`docs/solver-inventory-2026-09/02-frontend-ir-and-rewriting.md`).

## Schema

### A. Input front end

**Exactly two input languages: SMT-LIB 2.6 and SyGuS 2.1.** `enum
InputLanguage` has three values — `SMT_LIB_2_6`, `SYGUS_2_1`, `UNKNOWN`
(`include/cvc5/cvc5_types.h:684-696`) — and `Parser::mkParser`
(`src/parser/parser.cpp:117-142`) builds an `Smt2Parser` for both (SyGuS is an
`isSygus` flag on the same parser), `Unhandled()` otherwise.

**There is no TPTP parser.** `find src -iname "*tptp*"` returns nothing; TPTP
survives only as an *output* dialect
(`Language::LANG_SMTLIB_V2_6_TPTP`, `src/options/language.h:34-35`). If you
have read that cvc5 accepts TPTP, that is no longer true at this commit.

**ANTLR is gone and there is no Flex either.** `find src/parser -name 'flex*'`
returns 0 and `src/parser/` has exactly one language subdirectory, `smt2/`.
The stack is a hand-written lexer plus a recursive-descent parser:
`src/parser/lexer.{h,cpp}` + `tokens.{h,cpp}` (generic), `smt2/smt2_lexer.{h,cpp}`,
`smt2/smt2_parser.{h,cpp}`, `smt2/smt2_cmd_parser.{h,cpp}`,
`smt2/smt2_term_parser.{h,cpp}`, `smt2/smt2_state.{h,cpp}`. The only textual
mention of ANTLR left in the tree is a stale comment
(`src/include/cvc5parser_private.h:24`).

**Commands**: `Smt2CmdParser::parseNextCommand` has 64 `case Token::` labels, one
of which is `EOF_TOK`, so **63 command tokens** are handled
(`src/parser/smt2/smt2_cmd_parser.cpp:128-949`); `src/parser/commands.h` declares
46 `Cmd` subclasses. Beyond standard SMT-LIB: `declare-heap`,
`declare-oracle-fun`, `declare-pool`, `declare-sort-parameter`,
`get-abduct(-next)`, `get-interpol(-next)`, `get-difficulty`,
`get-learned-literals`, `get-timeout-core(-assuming)`, `get-unsat-core-lemmas`,
`get-model-domain-elements`, `simplify`, `synth-fun`/`synth-inv`,
`find-synth(-next)`, `check-synth(-next)`, `inv-constraint`, `set-feature`.

**Rejected vs silently accepted** — the distinction this axis asks for:

| Input | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Unknown command | **Hard parse error** — `default: d_lex.unexpectedTokenError(...)` (`smt2_cmd_parser.cpp:952-954`) |
| `set-option` with an unknown key | **Not** a parse error; the command is built and `SetOptionCommand::invoke` reports `CommandUnsupported`/`CommandRecoverableFailure` (`src/parser/commands.cpp:2636-2655`) |
| Missing `set-logic` | **Silently repaired**: four warnings, then `setLogic("ALL")` — only strict mode makes it an error (`smt2/smt2_state.cpp:1055-1081`) |
| A second `set-logic` | parse error (`smt2_state.cpp:754-756`) |
| `set-logic` under `--force-logic` | **Silently ignored** — becomes an `EmptyCommand` (`smt2_cmd_parser.cpp:862-873`) |
| Symbol outside the declared logic | parse error at the term-construction site (`smt2_state.cpp:495-589`) |
| SyGuS `set-feature` with an unknown key | **Warning only**, becomes `EmptyCommand` (`smt2_cmd_parser.cpp:834-848`) |
| `(set-option :use-portfolio ...)` | hard parse error (`smt2_cmd_parser.cpp:892-895`) |

`--parsing-mode` ∈ {`default`, `strict`, `lenient`}, default `default`
(`src/options/parser_options.toml:4-29`); `--strict-parsing` is an alias.

Option *combinations* are policed elsewhere, imperatively, in
`src/smt/set_defaults.cpp` — some throw `FatalOptionException` (e.g.
`--ackermann` with `--incremental`, `:1223`), others are **silently forced off**
with only a `-o options` notification (`unconstrainedSimp` `:1235`,
`sortInference` `:1290`, `arithMLTrick` `:1293`).

**API surface**: `include/cvc5/cvc5.h` (7,116 lines) / `src/api/cpp/cvc5.cpp`
(9,151). First-party bindings under `src/api/`: C (`c/cvc5.cpp`, 5,912 lines),
Python (Cython: `python/cvc5.pxi`, 6,237 lines), Java (32 `.java` + 23 JNI
`.cpp`).

Against our side: our parser also rejects unknown commands
(`crates/axeyum-smtlib/src/parse.rs:6297`), which is the same posture; our
silent-omission case is `set-option`, recorded and never read
(`docs/solver-inventory-2026-09/02-frontend-ir-and-rewriting.md`). The
divergence worth noting is the opposite one: cvc5 **silently repairs** a missing
`set-logic` to `ALL`, and our front door decides routes from the declared logic.

### B. Preprocessing (before search)

**37 passes, 37 registered names, 1:1** — `src/preprocessing/passes/*.cpp`,
registered in `src/preprocessing/preprocessing_pass_registry.cpp:125-163`.
The registry is a flat `name → constructor` map and carries **no ordering**;
`ProcessAssertions::finishInit` instantiates all 37 eagerly
(`src/smt/process_assertions.cpp:216-226`).

The order is hard-coded straight-line C++ in `ProcessAssertions::apply`
(`src/smt/process_assertions.cpp:90-402`) with a nested
`simplifyAssertions` (`:405-486`). Abridged sequence, in code order:

`bv-gauss` → `apply-substs` → `global-negate` → `nl-ext-purify` →
`real-to-int` → `ackermann` → `int-to-bv` → `ext-rew-pre` →
`rewrite`+`unconstrained-simplifier` → `bv-intro-pow2` → `bv-to-bool` →
`bv-to-int` → `foreign-theory-rewrite` → `distinct-elim` → **`rewrite`
(unconditional)** → `bool-to-bv` → `sep-skolem-emp` →
[quantified: `quantifiers-preprocess`, `fun-def-fmf`, `apply-substs`] →
[`strings-eager-pp` + `apply-substs`] → `sort-inference` →
`pseudo-boolean-processor` → `sygus-infer` → **`simplifyAssertions`**
(`non-clausal-simp` → `miplib-trick` → `ite-simp` →
`unconstrained-simplifier` → optional repeat) → `static-learning` →
`learned-rewrite` → [`ite-removal`+`apply-substs`] → optional repeat of
`simplifyAssertions` → `ho-elim` → `ff-disjunctive-bit` → `ff-bitsum` →
**`rewrite`** → **`static-rewrite`** → **`theory-preprocess`** →
`bv-eager-atoms`.

`synth-rr` is registered but never applied — positive control:
`grep -rn 'applyPass|"synth-rr"' src --include=*.cpp` outside
`process_assertions.cpp` returns only the registry line and the pass's own
constructor (`src/preprocessing/passes/synth_rew_rules.cpp:42`).

**Model reconstruction** is one mechanism: a user-context `TrustSubstitutionMap`
owned by `Env` (`src/smt/env.cpp:88-89`), reached through
`PreprocessingPassContext::getTopLevelSubstitutions`
(`src/preprocessing/preprocessing_pass_context.cpp:40-42`) and applied when a
model value is requested (`src/theory/theory_model.cpp:138`);
`--check-model` re-applies it (`src/smt/check_models.cpp:95`). Under
`--incremental` the map is instead pushed back into the assertion list as
ordinary equalities (`src/smt/assertion_pipeline.cpp:246-271`), flagged in
source as a known deficiency (`src/smt/preprocessor.cpp:71-72`).

**Proofs through preprocessing**: `src/smt/preprocess_proof_generator.{h,cpp}`
records `notifyNewAssert` / `notifyPreprocessed(n, np, pg)` and chains the
recorded equalities by transitivity, closing with `EQ_RESOLVE`
(`:106-111`). A pass that supplies no generator degrades to a `TrustId`
(`UNKNOWN_PREPROCESS`), i.e. a labeled hole rather than a checked step.

Contrast: our default pipeline is **five steps, run once**
(`canonicalize_terms` → `propagate_values` → `solve_eqs_bounded` →
`elim_unconstrained` → `canonicalize_terms`, `crates/axeyum-solver/src/auto.rs:2158-2199`),
with a second 8-round pipeline that the front door does not use
(`docs/solver-inventory-2026-09/02-frontend-ir-and-rewriting.md`, and gap #3 in
`11-wiring-and-integration.md`).

#### The term rewriter itself

`Rewriter::rewrite` (`src/theory/rewriter.cpp:109`) → `rewriteTo(theoryOf(node), node)`
(`:211`). It is **not recursive**: an explicit `std::deque<RewriteStackElement>`
(`:238`; a deque because each frame holds a live `NodeBuilder`) with five states
(`:54-58`) — `PRE_REWRITE`, `REWRITE_CHILDREN`, `POST_REWRITE`,
`WAIT_FOR_FULL_REWRITE`, `FINALIZE`.

The protocol is `RewriteResponse{status, node}` with
`RewriteStatus ∈ {REWRITE_DONE, REWRITE_AGAIN, REWRITE_AGAIN_FULL}`
(`src/theory/theory_rewriter.h:52-80`), and a proof-producing twin
`TrustRewriteResponse` (`:83`) with `preRewriteWithProof`/`postRewriteWithProof`
(`:129,:143`). The fixed-point loop (`rewriter.cpp:366-444`) re-applies
`postRewrite` until `REWRITE_DONE` with an unchanged kind, or restarts from a
new stack frame when the theory of the result changed or the status was
`REWRITE_AGAIN_FULL`.

**The cache is not a hash map**: two per-theory *node attributes*,
`RewriteAttibute<tid>::pre_rewrite` and `::post_rewrite`
(`src/theory/rewriter_attributes.h:27-32`), with a generated dispatch switch
(`src/theory/rewriter_tables_template.h:32-79`). Under proofs a cache hit is
only honored if `hasRewrittenWithProofs(node)` (`rewriter.cpp:231,:322`).
Non-termination is guarded under `CVC5_ASSERTIONS` by a `d_rewriteStack` set
membership test plus an idempotence re-check (`:403,:415-418,:426-429`).

**All 14 theories register a rewriter.** `THEORY_LAST` = 14
(`src/theory/theory_id.cpp:35-49`), all constructed unconditionally
(`src/smt/smt_solver.cpp:61-66`), each installing a `TheoryRewriter` through
`Rewriter::registerTheoryRewriter` (`rewriter.cpp:164`) — 14 concrete subclasses
plus `NoOpTheoryRewriter`.

Contrast with our `axeyum-rewrite`: 16 files, 16 passes, a rewrite manifest with
59 shipped rules all `Preservation::Denotation` + `ModelProjection::Identity`
(`canonical.rs:847-861`), and no per-theory rewriter registry — theory-specific
normalization happens inside each route or in `parse.rs` at lowering time. The
structural difference is that cvc5 has **one** rewriter with a per-theory
dispatch table and a hash-consed cache keyed on the node itself; we have a
pipeline of passes plus scattered per-route normalization. That is why our
rewriting is hard to audit as a whole and cvc5's fits in one 500-line file.

### C. Core SAT engine

Two selectable engines for CDCL(T) (`--sat-solver`, `src/options/prop_options.toml:5-15`,
default `CADICAL`, alternative `minisat`) and three for bit-blasting
(`--bv-sat-solver`, `src/options/bv_options.toml:5-18`, default `CADICAL`,
alternatives `cryptominisat`, `kissat`, both compile-time optional and reachable
*only* as BV back ends). Factory: `src/prop/sat_solver_factory.cpp:23-49`.

The MiniSat 2.2 fork (`src/prop/minisat/`) is still shipped and is **forced back
on under `--incremental`** unless the user set `--sat-solver`
(`src/smt/set_defaults.cpp:408-413`, reason string `"incremental"`).

**How CaDiCaL is integrated — the design we could adopt or reject.**
`src/prop/cadical/cdclt_propagator.h:23-25` declares
`class CadicalPropagator : public CaDiCaL::ExternalPropagator, public CaDiCaL::FixedAssignmentListener`
— i.e. CDCL(T) is built on CaDiCaL's **IPASIR-UP external-propagator API**, not
on a forked or reimplemented CDCL loop. Connected at
`src/prop/cadical/cadical.cpp:105-106`, only when a propagator exists; the
bit-blasting instance runs as a plain SAT solver with no propagator. Callbacks:
`notify_assignment`, `notify_fixed_assignment`, `notify_new_decision_level`,
`notify_backtrack`, `cb_check_found_model`, `cb_decide`, `cb_propagate`,
`cb_add_reason_clause_lit`, `cb_has_external_clause`,
`cb_add_external_clause_lit` (`cdclt_propagator.h:40-131`).

Consequences of that choice, all visible in source:

| Concern | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| Decision heuristic | Partly cvc5's: `cb_decide` (`cdclt_propagator.cpp:311-360`) → `TheoryProxy::getNextDecisionRequest` (`theory_proxy.cpp:380`) → theory engine first, then `src/decision/JustificationStrategy`. `--decision` defaults to `INTERNAL` but `set_defaults.cpp:1818-1889` upgrades it to `JUSTIFICATION` for `ALL`, quantified logics, strings, QF_BV, QF_AUFBV/QF_ABV/QF_UFBV, QF_AUFLIA, QF_LRA |
| Restart policy | **CaDiCaL's.** cvc5 sets exactly five CaDiCaL options (`cadical.cpp:95-104`: `quiet`, and in CDCL(T) mode `walk=0`, `lucky=0`, `ilb=0`, `ilbassumptions=0`) |
| Clause-DB reduction | **CaDiCaL's.** No cvc5-side deletion code |
| Phase saving | Preference pushed down via `preferPhase` → `CadicalPropagator::phase` (`cadical.cpp:353-357`) |
| Chronological backtracking | CaDiCaL's, unconfigured |
| push/pop | Emulated with one fresh **activation literal per user level**, assumed at every `solve()` (`cadical.cpp:159-166`, `:321-345`). A learned clause is guarded by the activation literal of the highest introduction level among its literals (`clause_user_level`, `cdclt_propagator.h:218-225`), so it survives pops of higher levels |
| Seed | `--seed` seeds cvc5's own `Random` (`src/smt/solver_engine.cpp:174`) and is **not forwarded to CaDiCaL**. `--sat-random-seed`, `--restart-int-base`, `--restart-int-inc`, `--random-freq` are MiniSat-only (`src/prop/minisat/minisat.cpp:156-167`) and silently inert under the default backend |

Two artifacts of the delegation worth recording as findings:

- `CadicalSolver::attachProofManager` is an **empty stub** (`cadical.cpp:316-319`,
  `// not implemented yet`) even though `PropEngine` attaches one unconditionally
  when `produceProofs` is set (`prop_engine.cpp:95-101`). Resolution-proof
  tracking (`sat_proof_manager.{h,cpp}`) now lives under `src/prop/minisat/` —
  it is a MiniSat-specific component. CaDiCaL proofs go through a separate
  LRUP `CaDiCaL::Tracer` (`src/prop/cadical/proof_tracer.h:36`), from which
  `CadicalSolver::getProof` builds a chain-resolution `ProofNode`
  (`cadical.cpp:388-397`).
- `CadicalSolver::getOrderHeap()` returns `{}` (`cadical.cpp:386`), so the
  `heap-scatter`/`heap-cube` partitioning strategies silently produce nothing
  under the default SAT backend (see O).

Against our side: our SAT core is our own CDCL with VSIDS, DRAT/LRAT output and
an in-tree checker (`docs/solver-inventory-2026-09/01-sat-core-and-cnf.md`;
ADR-0012, ADR-1703 retiring BatSat). cvc5's design is the opposite bet — buy the
SAT engine, spend the effort on the theory interface. The IPASIR-UP shape is the
part worth evaluating deliberately: it is what makes "theory propagation without
owning the CDCL loop" possible at all.

CNF: `src/prop/cnf_stream.cpp` is a standard Tseitin encoder (`handleXor` :318,
`handleOr` :337, `handleAnd` :370, `handleImplies` :404, `handleIff` :430,
`handleIte` :460, driven from `toCNF` :496 with an iterative worklist).
Top-level assertions bypass literal introduction (`convertAndAssertAnd`/`Or`/`Xor`,
:569/:600/:631). Literal reuse via the context-dependent cache
`d_nodeToLiteralMap` (`cnf_stream.h:78`).

### D. Inprocessing (during search)

Two cvc5-side mechanisms, both **off by default**, plus whatever CaDiCaL does
internally:

1. **Lemma inprocessing** — `src/prop/lemma_inprocess.{h,cpp}`.
   `--lemma-inprocess=MODE` default `none`, modes `full`/`light`
   (`src/options/theory_options.toml:106-122`). Applied in `PropEngine` just
   before a lemma is asserted (`src/prop/prop_engine.cpp:330-335`), substituting
   literals with level-zero-learned equalities held by
   `ZeroLevelLearner::getSimplifications()` so existing SAT literals are reused.
   Only lemmas flagged `LemmaProperty::INPROCESS` are eligible
   (`src/theory/lemma_property.h:34-35`); the only producer of that flag is
   quantifier instantiation (`src/theory/quantifiers/instantiate.cpp:331`).
2. **Deep restarts** — `--deep-restart=MODE` default `none`
   (`src/options/smt_options.toml:509-532`), driver
   `src/smt/smt_driver_deep_restarts.cpp`. On restart it harvests level-zero
   literals (`:41-42`) and re-runs the **entire** preprocessing pipeline with
   them added as assertions (`:52-105`). This is restart-scoped inprocessing,
   not in-search.

**Proofs do not survive either.** `set_defaults.cpp:1159-1165` declares lemma
inprocessing incompatible with proofs ("introduces dependencies from learned
unit literals that are not tracked"); `:1148-1152` says the same for deep
restarts. That is the honest version of the same trade we make: our
proof-carrying `axeyum-cnf/src/inprocess.rs` exists but has no caller outside
its crate's tests, and the shipping path hand-rolls the passes with a different
certificate mechanism (`docs/solver-inventory-2026-09/11-wiring-and-integration.md`,
gaps #1 and #2). Both projects have inprocessing that a proof-producing run does
not get; cvc5 refuses the combination in code, we default the whole thing off.

**No AIG and no ABC.** Positive control: `grep -rn 'aig|Aig|AIG'` over
`src/**.{cpp,h,toml}` returns 2 hits, both the substring inside the English word
"straightforward"; `grep -rn 'abc|ABC' src/theory/bv/` returns nothing.

`proof_bitblaster.*`, `bitblast_proof_generator.*`,

(`bitblast/bitblast_utils.h:57-140`) and hands them to `CnfStream::convertAndAssert`
(`src/theory/bv/bv_solver_bitblast.cpp:191`). Structural hashing exists but at
the term-DAG level: NodeManager hash-consing (`src/expr/node_manager_template.h:925,1037,1124-1141`)

`src/options/bv_options.toml:87-105`, which carries `no_support = ["proofs"]`):

- `bitblast` — owns a **separate** SAT solver + CnfStream (`bv_solver_bitblast.cpp:369-382`),
  asserts facts as assumptions and solves with `solve(assumptions)` (`:227`),
  reads conflicts from `getUnsatAssumptions` (`:242`). Incremental/lazy.
- `bitblast-internal` — no sub-solver; emits `atom ⟺ blasted-atom` as a lemma to

preprocessing pass under `--solve-bv-as-int` with

Against our side: we do have an AIG layer (`axeyum-aig`, deterministic
structural hashing) plus `IncrementalLowering`
(`docs/solver-inventory-2026-09/01-sat-core-and-cnf.md`). cvc5 does not, and
does not want one: its sharing comes from the global hash-consed term DAG.
That is a genuine architectural divergence, not a gap in either direction.

### G. Theory combination

**Care-graph Nelson-Oppen, and nothing else.** `--tc-mode` is typed `TcMode`
with **exactly one mode**, `care-graph`, defaulting to it
(`src/options/theory_options.toml:76-88`); `TheoryEngine::finishInit`
(`src/theory/theory_engine.cpp:132-141`) constructs `CombinationCareGraph` and
`Unimplemented()`s on anything else. There is **no** model-based theory
combination in this tree — the only occurrence of "CombinationModelBased" is a
stale comment at `src/theory/model_manager_distributed.cpp:40`. Positive
control: `CombinationCareGraph` is a real class at
`src/theory/combination_care_graph.h:32`.

**When it runs.** `TheoryEngine::check` (`theory_engine.cpp:486-505`) invokes
combination only at full effort and only once every theory has quiesced —
`logicInfo().isSharingEnabled() && !d_factsAsserted && !needCheck() && !d_inConflict`.

**How care pairs are computed.** `CombinationCareGraph::combineTheories`
(`src/theory/combination_care_graph.cpp:33-90`) builds a local `CareGraph`
(a `std::set<CarePair>`, `src/theory/care_graph.h:27-58`), calls
`t->getCareGraph(&careGraph)` on each **parametric** theory, and for each pair
emits a **split lemma** `(or (= a b) (not (= a b)))` through
`d_sharedSolver->sendLemma(..., InferenceId::COMBINATION_SPLIT)` (`:56-76`),
then `ensureLiteral` + `preferPhase(e, true)` (`:87-88`).

The **default** `Theory::computeCareGraph` (`src/theory/theory.cpp:250-280`) is
the naive quadratic one: all same-typed shared-term pairs whose
`getEqualityStatus` is not already `EQUALITY_{TRUE,FALSE}_AND_PROPAGATED`. Six
theories override it with operator-indexed `TNodeTrie` traversal instead —
`uf` (`theory_uf.cpp:582`), `datatypes` (`:1060`), `bags` (`:524`), `strings`
(`:1087`), `sets` (`theory_sets_private.cpp:1434`), `arrays` (`:1030`) —
using `addCarePairArgs`/`processCarePairArgs` (`theory.cpp:430-463`) and
`CarePairArgumentCallback` (`care_pair_argument_callback.cpp:37`).

**Eight parametric theories** participate (`TheoryTraits<T>::isParametric`,
filtered at `theory_engine.cpp:122-126`, sourced from `properties = [...
"parametric" ...]` in each `kinds.toml`): `uf`, `arrays`, `datatypes`, `sets`,
`bags`, `sep`, `strings`, `ff`.

**Two paths to the SAT solver**, and the distinction matters: care pairs become
*split lemmas*; already-entailed interface equalities are instead propagated as
*shared literals* by `SharedTermsDatabase`'s own equality engine, reported under
`THEORY_BUILTIN` (`shared_solver_distributed.cpp:35-89`). Those propagated pairs
are exactly the ones the default `computeCareGraph` skips.

**Equality-engine sharing**: `--ee-mode` defaults to **`distributed`** (each
theory keeps its own engine); `central` exists and is not the default
(`theory_options.toml:62-74`, `combination_engine.cpp:43-72`).
`EqEngineManagerCentral::usesCentralEqualityEngine`
(`ee_manager_central.cpp:202-213`) admits UF, DATATYPES, BAGS, FP, SETS,
STRINGS, SEP, ARRAYS, BV, and ARITH only when `arith.arithEqSolver` is on —
which `--ee-mode=central` turns on for you (`set_defaults.cpp:877-882`).

Our side: `theory_combination.rs` exists with four functions that have **zero**
callers (positive control: `classify_interface_equalities` has 23) —
`docs/solver-inventory-2026-09/00-README.md` §5. Combination in the shipping
path is Ackermann elimination (`euf.rs`, ADR-0013) plus per-pair online CDCL(T)
routes, not a general care-graph mechanism. Concretely: we have no
`CareGraph`, no shared-term registry with propagated interface equalities, and
no per-theory `computeCareGraph`, so every new theory pair we support costs a
hand-written combination route rather than an override of one method.

### I. Model production

**Build pipeline.** `TheoryEngine::check` at full/last-call effort calls
`d_tc->buildModel()` (`theory_engine.cpp:542,568,719,729`) →
`ModelManager::buildModel` (`src/theory/model_manager.cpp:73-116`), which
memoizes the result and **disables the resource manager for the duration**
(`rm->setEnabled(false)` at `:86`, re-enabled at `:112` — model construction is
outside the budget). `ModelManagerDistributed::prepareModel` then walks the
theories in `CVC5_FOR_EACH_THEORY` order calling
`collectAssertedTermsForModel` + `collectModelInfo`
(`model_manager_distributed.cpp:93-96`); `Theory::collectModelInfo`
(`theory.cpp:300-315`) asserts the theory's equality engine into the model and
calls the per-theory `collectModelValues` hook, which 12 theories override.

**Representative selection.** `TheoryEngineModelBuilder::buildModel`
(`theory_model_builder.cpp:385`) partitions equivalence classes into three
`TypeSet`s (constant reps, theory-asserted reps, no rep, `:415-425`) and orders
types so component types precede container types (`:426-428`). Classes are
classified assignable vs **evaluable**, and evaluable classes are never assigned
— they are computed by `evaluateEqc`/`normalize` (`:955-961`, `:72`, `:1246`).
The assignment loop (`:948-1077`) uses an `Assigner` with an excluded-value set
for finite types, otherwise a `TypeEnumerator`. Codatatype and
uninterpreted-sort exclusions are `isExcludedCdtValue` (`:211`) and
`isExcludedUSortValue` (`:309`).

**Uninterpreted-sort cardinality** is enforced through
`TypeEnumeratorProperties` with `tepFixUSortCard = options().quantifiers.finiteModelFind`
(`:406-408`); with FMF on the enumerator only yields legal constants, and
`isExcludedUSortValue` survives only as a `CVC5_ASSERTIONS` check
(`:1020-1041`, the source comment says "this is just an assertion now"). The
cardinality *solver* is `uf/cardinality_extension.cpp`.

**Function values.** `--assign-function-values` defaults **true**
(`src/options/theory_options.toml:20-25`; forced true for higher-order logic).
First-order functions become a `LAMBDA` over a nested-`ITE` body built from a
`UfModelTree` (`theory_model.cpp:848-850`; `uf/theory_uf_model.cpp:99,251-259`);
higher-order functions are built from curried `HO_APPLY` collection
(`theory_model.cpp:975-978`); arrays are `STORE`/`STORE_ALL` constants
(`arrays/theory_arrays.cpp:1153`).

**Does cvc5 check its own models by default? No.** `--check-models` is
`default = "false"` (`src/options/smt_options.toml:63-69`); `CheckModels` is only
constructed when the option is set (`src/smt/solver_engine.cpp:235-238`).
Positive control that opt-in is the house style: `--check-proofs` and
`--check-unsat-cores` are likewise `default = "false"`
(`smt_options.toml:153-158,203-208`).

And when it does run, `CheckModels::checkModel`
(`src/smt/check_models.cpp:66-230`) is deliberately lenient in four places
worth naming, because they are exactly the failure modes a checker that cannot
fail exhibits:

- it **refuses outright** on separation-logic heaps (`:76-81`);
- under `--fmf-fun` a hard failure is **downgraded to a warning** (`:82-91`);
- a non-constant value falls back to spawning a **subsolver `checkSat`** on the
  residual and accepting `SAT` (`--check-model-subsolver`, default **true**,
  `:167-185`);
- if that is not possible it emits "cannot check simplified assertion" as a
  **warning, not an error** (`:187-193`).

Only an assertion that evaluates to literal `false` produces the error at
`:200-230`.

Against our side, this is the sharpest posture difference in the whole
comparison, and it is now sharper than the option default alone suggests.
CLAUDE.md makes model replay a **hard rule** ("Every `sat` result must be
checkable by evaluating the original term against the lifted model"), and
`docs/solver-inventory-2026-09/08-models-proofs-and-evidence.md` records ~13
hand-written enforcement sites with `replay_model`/`check_model` WIRED into the
default path. cvc5 checks models when asked, and the check it then runs has
three documented escape hatches. This is the one axis where our discipline is
unambiguously stronger, and it is worth saying that our own contributor guide's
rule — make the exit status depend on the finding — is the rule cvc5's
`checkModel` breaks and our model replay keeps.

Also relevant: `--produce-models` is `default = "false"`
(`smt_options.toml:53-60`); `--model-cores` (`:96-101`) drives
`ModelCoreBuilder`; `src/smt/model_blocker.cpp` implements `block-model`.

### L. Interpolation

Supported, via **SyGuS synthesis to an internal subsolver** — not Craig
interpolation off a proof. `src/smt/interpolation_solver.{h,cpp}` (233 lines)
delegates to `src/theory/quantifiers/sygus/sygus_interpol.cpp`
(`setSynthGrammar` :176, `mkSygusConjecture` :240, `solveInterpolation` :344,
`solveInterpolationNext` :392). The shared-symbol requirement is enforced by
splitting the top-level substitution map — a substitution `x → t` is applied
only if every symbol of `(= x t)` also occurs in the goal, otherwise it is
demoted to an ordinary axiom (`interpolation_solver.cpp:58-99`) — and the
applied part is conjoined back onto the result (`:117-120`).
`--interpolants-mode={default,assumptions,conjecture,shared,all}`
(`src/options/smt_options.toml:461-483`).

**Guarantee**: none from the procedure. `--check-interpolants`
(`smt_options.toml:494`) runs a two-phase a-posteriori check (`A ⇒ I` and
`I ⇒ B`, each by refuting the negation, `interpolation_solver.cpp:176-215`).
Failure to synthesize is reported as "no interpolant produced", not as "none
exists" — the procedure is incomplete. API: `include/cvc5/cvc5.h:6457,6492,6518`.

Abduction is the same shape: `src/smt/abduction_solver.cpp:36-94` builds a SyGuS
conjecture with `SygusAbduct::mkAbductionConjecture` (:65) and reads the
solution back from a fresh subsolver (:120-125); `--check-abducts` checks it
(`:170-240`). API at `cvc5.h:6543,6569,6595`.

Ours are nine **proof-based** interpolators — Farkas for conjunctive QF_LRA,
McMillan-2003 off the LRAT proof for propositional and disjunctive QF_LRA/LIA,
relax-then-integer-recheck for QF_LIA, Ackermannize-then-LRA for QF_UFLRA,
joint-bit-blast-then-lift for QF_BV — each with a `verify_interpolant`
(`docs/solver-inventory-2026-09/08-models-proofs-and-evidence.md:436-444`).
This is one of the clearest rows in the "we have, they do not" table, with the
caveat that all seven of our `*_certified` variants have no caller.

### N. Incrementality

`ContextManager` (`src/smt/context_manager.h:36`) makes one global push at
`setup()` and pushes/pops a context around a check-sat that carries assumptions
(`:46-71`); pops are deferred and drained by `doPendingPops()` (`:83-88`).
Assertions live in user-context `CDList`s (`src/smt/assertions.cpp:38-39`), so
they survive `check-sat` and are undone by `pop`. Top-level substitutions are
also on the user context (`src/smt/env.cpp:88-89`). At the SAT layer, push/pop
is the activation-literal scheme described in C.

What `--incremental` forces off (`SetDefaults::incompatibleWithIncremental`,
`src/smt/set_defaults.cpp:1213-1293`):

- **Hard errors**: separation-logic heap (:1218), `--ackermann` (:1223), eager
  bit-blasting outside pure BV (:1237), `--solve-int-as-bv` (:1266),
  `--deep-restart` (:1271), `--compute-partitions > 1` (:1276), `--proof-log`
  (:1283).
- **Silently forced off**: `unconstrainedSimp` (:1235), `sygusInference` (:1252),
  `sygusInst` (:1264), `sortInference` (:1290), `globalNegate` (:1291),
  `cegqiNestedQE` (:1292), the MIPLIB trick (:1293).
- **And the SAT backend flips to MiniSat** (:408-413).

### O. Parallelism

Two unrelated mechanisms, neither shared-memory threaded solving.

1. **Portfolio** — `src/main/portfolio_driver.{h,cpp}`, `--use-portfolio`
   (default false), `--portfolio-jobs=n` (default **1**),
   `--portfolio-dry-run` (`src/options/main_options.toml:197-218`).
   Implementation is **`fork()`-based process parallelism**: `fork()` at
   `portfolio_driver.cpp:496` (worker) and `:555` (per-strategy timeout child),
   POSIX pipes for output capture (`:225`, `:267-283`), reaping at `:465-630`.
   Strategies are logic-keyed option sets with relative timeout fractions
   (`portfolio_driver.h:117-191`). It lives in `src/main/`, so it is
   **CLI-only** — not reachable through the C++/Python API.
2. **Cube-and-conquer partitioning** — `src/theory/partition_generator.{h,cpp}`,
   a `TheoryEngineModule`. `--compute-partitions=N` (default 0),
   `--partition-strategy` default `decision-scatter` with six modes
   (`decision-scatter`, `heap-scatter`, `lemma-scatter`, `decision-cube`,
   `heap-cube`, `lemma-cube`), `--partition-when={tlimit,climit}`,
   `--partition-conflict-size`, `--write-partitions-to`,
   `--append-learned-literals-to-cubes`, `--random-partitioning`
   (`src/options/parallel_options.toml`). Scatter emits `C1`, `¬C1 ∧ C2`, … 
   (`makeScatterPartitions`, `partition_generator.h:98`); cube emits mutually
   exclusive cubes (`:114`). **Partitions are written out, not solved** — the
   parallel execution is the caller's problem.

   Defect worth recording: `heap-scatter`/`heap-cube` read
   `PropEngine::getPropOrderHeap()` → `d_satSolver->getOrderHeap()`
   (`prop_engine.cpp:403`), and `CadicalSolver::getOrderHeap()` returns `{}`
   (`cadical.cpp:386`). Since `--sat-solver` defaults to `cadical`, heap-based
   partitioning silently produces nothing; and `--compute-partitions > 1` is a
   hard error under `--incremental`, which is the one mode that would have
   forced MiniSat.

Ours: `portfolio.rs` with `FusedGroup`, runtime-dead by default
(`AXEYUM_PORTFOLIO_WORKERS` defaults to 1 — integration gap #8).
Neither project ships parallel solving on by default.

## Not comparable

Four places where a feature-by-feature row would mislead.

**1. The strings ladders are different kinds of object.** cvc5's 20 `InferStep`s
run inside one CDCL(T) theory check; each step may emit lemmas or a conflict
that the SAT solver then explores, and the loop runs to fixpoint across many
`check()` calls. Our 13 stages are alternative whole-query routes tried in
sequence, each of which may only upgrade an `Unknown`. Counting "13 stages vs 20
steps" invites the reading that we are 65% of the way there; the correct reading
is that we have a route ladder and they have a theory solver, and the two do not
convert. The place they meet is our online CDCL(T) string route
(`string_theory.rs`, stage 8), which is the only stage architecturally
comparable to cvc5's loop — and it carries 4 inference rules against their 87.

**2. Extended-function reduction targets diverge, so operator tables lie.**
cvc5 reduces `str.replace_all`, `str.indexof_re`, `str.to_lower`, `str.rev` etc.
to **bounded-quantified arithmetic** and discharges them with
`BoundedIntegers` + `ModelEngine` — which is why enabling strings force-enables
two quantifier modules. We reduce the same operators to **bounded bit-vectors**
inside the parser (`parse.rs:8329-10249`) and discharge them with the BV
solver. A row saying "both support `str.replace_all`" is true and nearly
content-free: theirs is unbounded and quantifier-backed, ours is bounded by
`STRING_BOUND_CAP` and complete only within the declared window. The honest
statement is that cvc5 is complete on a fragment where we are complete only up
to a bound, and we decide some bounded instances faster because a BV solver is
a better fit for them.

**3. "Proof coverage" is not one axis.** cvc5 covers many more *rules*
(172 with 143 checked) and prints four formats; we cover fewer rules but carry
two things cvc5 does not — an in-process dependent-type kernel that re-derives
the proof as terms, and a self-rechecking `Evidence` envelope for `sat` as well
as `unsat`. Comparing rule counts alone would say cvc5 wins 172 to 29;
comparing "can a referee check this with a small trusted base" would say
something else. Both numbers are true and neither is the whole answer.

**4. The SAT engine is a bought component for cvc5 and the product for us.**
cvc5 sets five CaDiCaL options and owns no restart, reduction, or phase policy.
Rows like "restart policy" therefore have no cvc5 answer that means anything —
the answer is "CaDiCaL's, unconfigured". The comparison that *is* meaningful is
architectural: IPASIR-UP lets cvc5 get theory propagation and theory-driven
decisions without owning the CDCL loop, and that interface is the thing to
evaluate on its merits, separately from any performance claim. Under
ADR-1703 our own core is settled identity, so the question is not "should we
switch engines" — it is whether an IPASIR-UP-shaped interface is the right seam
between our CDCL core and our theory solvers.
