**Z3 has a role in this comparison that no other solver here has.** It is our
differential oracle. [ADR-0002](../research/09-decisions/adr-0002-ground-up-identity-oracle-bootstrap.md)
makes it bootstrap scaffolding with a planned demotion path — "M0 backend →
differential oracle → CI cross-check" — where each demotion happens "when the
evidence pipeline replaces the trust the oracle was providing". So this file has
two jobs: the sixteen-axis inventory, and an honest answer to *what we currently
depend on it for*. The second is
[§ What our differential suites actually use Z3 for](#what-our-differential-suites-actually-use-z3-for),
and its answer is far narrower than the ADR's framing implies.

Coverage is deliberately uneven, in the priority the brief set: Axis B
(tactics/preprocessing) and Axis G (theory combination) are read deeply; F, H,
J, K, L, M in the middle; A, C, D, E, I, N, O, P surveyed.

## Summary

- **Z3 does not have one preprocessing framework. It has three, and they
  overlap by pass name.** (1) `asserted_formulas::reduce` — a fixed 21-step
  sequence inside the legacy `smt::context`
  (`src/solver/assertions/asserted_formulas.cpp:267-309`); (2) the tactic
  framework — **118** `ADD_TACTIC` registrations, **42** `ADD_PROBE`,
  user-addressable from SMT-LIB via `check-sat-using` / `apply` /
  `declare-tactic`; (3) the newer `src/ast/simplifiers/` layer — **42**
  `dependent_expr_simplifier` subclasses, **39** `ADD_SIMPLIFIER` — sequenced by
  `init_preprocess` (`src/solver/solver_preprocess.cpp:48-91`) and driven by
  `simplifier_solver.cpp:165`. `propagate_values`, `solve_eqs`,
  `elim_unconstrained` and `max_bv_sharing` each appear in more than one of the
  three, and some tactics (`elim-uncnstr` vs `elim-uncnstr2`,
  `propagate-values` vs `propagate-values2`, `reduce-args` vs `reduce-args2`)
  are two live implementations of the same pass under different names. Any
  claim of the form "Z3's preprocessing does X" must say which framework.
- **The default *solver* preprocessing pipeline is nine passes, and its first
  four are ours minus one, in our order.** With `smt_params` defaults
  (`src/params/preprocessor_params.h:35-52`) the enabled sequence from
  `init_preprocess` is `rewriter` → `propagate_values` → `solve_eqs` →
  `elim_unconstrained` → `cnf_nnf` → `max_bv_sharing` → `refine_inj_axiom` →
  `bound_simplifier` → `flatten_clauses`. Ours is `canonicalize_terms` →
  `propagate_values` → `solve_eqs_bounded` → `elim_unconstrained` →
  `canonicalize_terms` (`auto.rs:2158-2199`,
  [`02-frontend-ir-and-rewriting.md`](../solver-inventory-2026-09/02-frontend-ir-and-rewriting.md)).
  **The gap is not the pass list. It is that theirs is assembled from a registry
  of interchangeable, individually-parameterised objects and ours is a function
  body.**
- **The tactic framework's model-conversion half is alive; its proof-conversion
  half is not.** A `goal` carries three converters (`src/tactic/goal.h:54-56`):
  model, proof, dependency. `model_converter` appears in **52** files under
  `src/tactic/` + `src/ast/simplifiers/`. `proof_converter` appears in **four**,
  three of which are the framework itself; **exactly one tactic populates one**
  (`src/tactic/core/split_clause_tactic.cpp:113,125`), plus `solver2tactic.cpp:120-121`
  passing through an underlying solver's proof — against **112** `mk_*_tactic`
  factories in the tree.
- **Turning proofs on turns the solver off, in three separate ways.**
  (a) **20 tactics throw** `fail_if_proof_generation` (`src/tactic/tactic.cpp:284-290`)
  — including `sat`, `nlsat`, `aig`, `fm`, `pb2bv`, `lia2pb`, `nla2bv`,
  `tseitin-cnf`, `ackermannize_bv`, and `par_and_then` itself. (b) **34 of 42
  simplifiers become silent no-ops**: `dependent_expr_state.h:243` makes
  `supports_proofs()` default to `false`, and
  `dependent_expr_state_tactic.h:132` reads
  `if (!in->proofs_enabled() || m_simp->supports_proofs()) m_simp->reduce();`
  — no exception, no warning. (c) The **QF_BV pipeline changes engine
  entirely**: `qfbv_tactic.cpp:126-130` routes to `simplify; smt` under
  `mk_produce_proofs_probe()` instead of the SAT core, and `if_no_proofs`
  strips the AIG pass at `:116`. Z3's own release notes name the consequence:
  "integration of pre-processing proofs with logging proofs. There is currently
  no supported bridge to create a end-to-end proof objects"
  (`RELEASE_NOTES.md:653-654`).
- **The default arithmetic solver emits theory lemmas with no Farkas
  coefficients at all.** `smt.arith.solver` defaults to 6 = `theory_lra`
  (`src/params/theory_arith_params.h:57`, `AS_NEW_ARITH`;
  `smt_setup.cpp:600-606`). `theory_lra`'s `vector<parameter> m_params`
  (`theory_lra.cpp:3650`) is **reset** (`:3655`), **traced** (`:3713`) and
  **passed out** (`:2078, :2541, :2584, :3252, :3731`) — and never populated:
  `grep 'm_params\.push_back\|m_params\.append' src/smt/theory_lra.cpp` returns
  nothing (positive control: the legacy `theory_arith_core.h:1099` builds
  `parameter coeffs[3] = { parameter(symbol("farkas")), … }`). The only
  `farkas` in `theory_lra` is a static `{farkas, 1, 1}` for bound-ordering
  axioms (`:2908-2909`). And `setup_QF_LRA` does **not** switch to the legacy
  solver when proofs are on (`smt_setup.cpp:428-442`; contrast QF_IDL/QF_RDL at
  `:291-293, :359-361`). So a QF_LRA refutation's arithmetic core is a bare
  `(th-lemma arith false …)`.
- **Z3's proofs are weaker than the reputation, and the project says so.** The
  legacy format has **43** rule kinds (`src/ast/ast.h:1186-1195`, 44 tokens
  including the `PR_UNDEF` sentinel), of which `PR_TH_LEMMA` is documented as
  "Generic proof for theory lemmas" carrying at most a hint string — `farkas`,
  `triangle-eq`, `gcd-test` for arithmetic, **nothing for any other theory**
  (`src/api/z3_api.h:730-745`) — and `PR_REWRITE` is one opaque step with "no
  antecedents" over the whole `th_rewriter` (`z3_api.h:507-519`). The newer
  `sat.euf` clause log's self-validator has a bit-vector plugin that is **six
  functions all returning `true`** (`src/sat/smt/bv_theory_checker.cpp:33-63`,
  comment at `:31`: "To bypass theory checking we pretend it is trusted"), a
  `distinct` plugin that is one `return true` (`distinct_theory_checker.h:42`),
  no custom validator for arrays/datatypes/quantifiers/FP, and a fallback that
  **calls Z3 on the lemma** — which the release notes flag as "generally
  insufficient as generated clauses are in principle required to only be
  satisfiability preserving" (`RELEASE_NOTES.md:675-677`). The legacy checker
  is worse: it is gated on `smt_params::m_check_proof` (`src/params/smt_params.h:87`),
  which **has no registered parameter and cannot be enabled without
  recompiling**.
- **The internal DRAT checker is RUP-only.** `drat::is_drat(unsigned, literal
  const*)` begins with an unconditional `return false;`
  (`src/sat/sat_drat.cpp:354`), making the RAT check below it dead code. This is
  consistent by design rather than a bug: the passes that would need RAT (BVE,
  blocked-clause elimination) emit **nothing** to DRAT and instead record
  `sat_model_converter` entries (`sat_simplifier.cpp:1591-1607, :1995-1997`),
  so soundness is preserved on the model side, not the proof side.
- **Interpolation is gone.** `RELEASE_NOTES.md:988-990` lists `interpolation
  API` first under "Removed features". `Z3_get_interpolant`,
  `Z3_compute_interpolant` have zero occurrences anywhere; no file matches
  `*iz3*`; `grep -rn 'interpolant' src/api/` is empty (positive control: the
  same grep for `optimize` finds `src/api/api_opt.cpp`). What survives is
  `qe::interpolator` (`src/qe/qe_mbi.h:150`) — model-based interpolation for
  propositional and UFLIA — reachable only through a **debug** command
  (`src/cmd_context/extra_cmds/dbg_cmds.cpp:382-386`). Its
  `validate_interpolant` returns `void` and prints a warning; it cannot fail.
- **Theory combination is model-based, implemented, and is exactly what our four
  uncalled `theory_combination.rs` functions were reaching for.**
  `context::is_shared` (`smt_context.cpp:4753-4826`) marks a node shared when it
  carries ≥2 theory variables, is an `ite`, has a parent from another theory
  family, or the owning theory says so; theories then propose equalities between
  shared variables whose *model values* coincide (`smt_theory.h:526`
  `assume_eqs(VarValueTable&)`, `theory_lra.cpp:1593-1627`), after
  `random_update()` (`theory_lra.cpp:1560-1591`) perturbs the LP solution to
  break accidental coincidences. Ours:
  `theory_combination::{shared_terms, propose_interface_equalities,
  combination_conflict, interface_th_eqs}` have **zero callers**
  ([`03-dispatch-routing-and-backends.md:387`](../solver-inventory-2026-09/03-dispatch-routing-and-backends.md)).
- **The theory-plugin contract is ~48 virtual methods, 6 of them pure**
  (`src/smt/smt_theory.h`): `internalize_atom` `:201`, `internalize_term`
  `:218`, `new_eq_eh` `:243`, `new_diseq_eh` `:256`, `display` `:471`,
  `mk_fresh` `:636`. Ours is 9 methods, 4 required (`euf_egraph.rs:67-162`).
  The four things theirs has that ours does not are **internalization**, **model
  construction** (`mk_value` `:606`), **shared-variable declaration**
  (`is_shared` `:337`) and **relevancy** (`relevant_eh` `:262`).
- **`final_check` is a rotating round-robin with level escalation**, not one
  pass: `context::final_check` (`smt_context.cpp:4218-4311`) cycles
  `m_final_check_idx` over theories *plus a quantifier slot*, keeps the index
  across calls for fairness, and re-runs the whole rotation at a higher `level`
  when a theory advertises `num_final_check_levels() > 1` — the only theory that
  does is `theory_lra` (`theory_lra.h:68`), whose level 2 invokes full `nlsat`.
- **Z3 does not validate its own model by default, and in the optimization path
  it cannot.** `smt_context.cpp:3632` guards validation on the legacy global
  `model_validate` (off by default, `src/params/context_params.cpp:164`); the
  base `theory::validate_model` is empty (`smt_theory.h:387`) and only **two**
  of ~24 theories override it (`theory_seq`, `theory_lra`); the deeper check
  beside it is `#if 0`'d (`smt_context.cpp:3639-3665`); `context::validate_model`
  returns `true` — "valid" — whenever evaluation *fails*
  (`smt_context_inv.cpp:372, :380`); `cmd_context::validate_model` skips
  quantified assertions and everything containing an array or underspecified
  operator (`cmd_context.cpp:2093-2131, :2232-2248`); and
  `opt::context::validate_model()` opens with a bare `return;`
  (`src/opt/opt_context.cpp:2006-2008`) while still being called from `:437`.
- **The tactic pipeline is only available on one-shot queries.**
  `combined_solver` (`src/solver/combined_solver.cpp:29-45`) holds solver1
  (tactic, non-incremental) and solver2 (`smt_context`, incremental) and
  **permanently** switches to solver2 on `push`, on `pop`, on an assertion after
  a `check-sat`, or when assumptions are supplied (`:162-195`, `:210-219`). Both
  solvers receive every assertion (`:160-168`), so assertion cost is paid twice.
  On `pop` below a base level, learned lemmas are deleted
  (`smt_context.cpp:2545-2551`).
- **What we actually use Z3 for is one function.** Across the whole workspace,
  every Z3 call reduces to: build terms, `Solver::new()`, `assert`, `check()`,
  read the `SatResult`. No tactics, goals, probes, unsat cores, proofs,
  assumptions, `Optimize`, or `push`/`pop`. Detail and controls in
  [§ What our differential suites actually use Z3 for](#what-our-differential-suites-actually-use-z3-for).

### B. Preprocessing (before search) — **the deep axis**

**Three frameworks.**

**(1) `asserted_formulas` — the legacy in-`smt_context` pipeline.**
`asserted_formulas::reduce()` (`src/solver/assertions/asserted_formulas.cpp:267`)
is a fixed sequence of `invoke(...)` calls, each aborting the rest on `false`:

```
propagate_values, find_macros, nnf_cnf, reduce_asserted_formulas,
pull_nested_quantifiers, lift_ite, ng_lift_ite, elim_term_ite, qe_lite,
refine_inj_axiom, distribute_forall, find_macros, apply_quasi_macros,
apply_bit2int, bv_size_reduce, cheap_quant_fourier_motzkin,
pattern_inference, max_bv_sharing_fn, elim_bvs_from_quantifiers,
reduce_asserted_formulas, flatten_clauses
```
(`:285-308`). A trailing second `propagate_values` is commented out at `:309`.
Early exit if there are no quantifiers and `!m_smt_params.m_preprocess`
(`:274-275`).

**(2) The tactic framework.** Four pure virtuals
(`src/tactic/tactic.h`): `operator()(goal_ref const& in, goal_ref_buffer& result)`
`:61`, `cleanup()` `:65`, `translate(ast_manager&)` `:73`, `name()` `:90`; plus
optional `updt_params` `:40`, `collect_param_descrs` `:41`,
`collect_statistics` `:63`, `set_logic` `:69`. A tactic maps one goal to a
*buffer* of subgoals, so branching (`or_else`, `split_clause`, `par_or`) is in
the type. Its own doc comment disclaims the published design: "this signature is
not compatible with the one described in the paper: 'The Strategy Challenge in
SMT Solving' … it would require a lot of re-engineering" (`tactic.h:52-59`).

A `goal` (`src/tactic/goal.h:41`) is three parallel arrays — formulas `m_forms`,
**per-formula proofs** `m_proofs`, dependencies `m_dependencies` (`:60-62`) —
three mode bits `m_models_enabled` `:64` / `m_proofs_enabled` `:65` /
`m_core_enabled` `:66`, a 2-bit **precision** lattice `PRECISE / UNDER / OVER /
UNDER_OVER` (`:43-48`), and **three converters**: `model_converter m_mc` `:54`,
`proof_converter m_pc` `:55`, `dependency_converter m_dc` `:56`, each with
`add()` (compose) and `set()` (replace) at `:158-163`.

Two structural facts follow:

- **Solved states are encoded in the goal, not signalled out of band.**
  `is_decided_sat()` is `size()==0 && sat_preserved()` (`goal.cpp:642`);
  `is_decided_unsat()` is `inconsistent() && unsat_preserved()` (`:646`). This is
  what lets `and_then` detect completion without a protocol.
- **The precision lattice is how under-approximating passes stay sound.**
  `nla2bv` calls `set_satisfiability_preserving(false)` whenever it adds a side
  condition (`nla2bv_tactic.cpp:172-181`) and then `g.updt_prec(goal::UNDER)`
  (`:116-117`); the QF_NIA pipeline follows it with `fail-if-undecided`
  (`qfnia_tactic.cpp:104`). We have no equivalent of this lattice.

**Model reconstruction, exactly.** `model_converter`'s contract is stated as
`m |= F_s iff mc(m) |= F` (`src/ast/converters/model_converter.h:26`).
`concat(mc1, mc2)` applies **mc2 first, then mc1**
(`model_converter.cpp:91-94`), and `goal::add(mc)` does
`m_mc = concat(m_mc.get(), mc)` (`goal.h:159`) — so the *newest* converter runs
*first*, unwinding transformations in reverse chronological order. The workhorse
is `generic_model_converter`, a list of `HIDE`/`ADD` instructions replayed in
reverse (`generic_model_converter.cpp:38-100`, reverse loop at `:48`), with an
evaluator-cache invalidation whenever an existing interpretation is overwritten
(`:88-92`). **`and_then` never mentions `mc()`**: composition is a side effect of
passes mutating the goal in place, and of `goal::copy_to` copying the converter
*pointers* so branches share a prefix and diverge (`goal.cpp:112-114`).

Registered surface: **118 `ADD_TACTIC`**, **42 `ADD_PROBE`**, **39
`ADD_SIMPLIFIER`** across `src/`. The probes are goal-feature predicates —
`is-qfbv`, `is-qflia`, `is-qfnra`, `arith-max-deg`, `arith-avg-bw`,
`num-bv-consts`, `has-quantifiers`, `has-patterns`, `produce-proofs`,
`is-quasi-pb`, `ackr-bound-probe`, `memory`, `depth`, `size`, … — the direct
analogue of our 11-field boolean `Features` scan (`auto.rs:9422-9452`), except
there are 42 of them and most are numeric, not boolean.

**(3) `src/ast/simplifiers/` — the newer layer, and the one the default
incremental solver uses.** 42 `dependent_expr_simplifier` subclasses. The
interface (`dependent_expr_state.h:234-243`) is `name()`, `push()`, `pop(n)`,

processed prefix (`:69-70, :90`), declaration freezing (`:52-54, :96-100`), and

new assertion mentions a substituted variable. At the boundary it lowers to
framework (2)'s representation: `get_model_converter()` walks the trail into a
`generic_model_converter`.

### D. Inprocessing (during search)

**Schedule.** `should_simplify()` is `m_conflicts_since_init >= m_next_simplify`
(`sat_solver.cpp:2058-2060`), checked in the search loop at `:1823` — after
conflict/propagate/cleanup/GC/rephase/restart, before `decide`. The three
constants that set the cadence are **deliberately unexposed**:
`next_simplify = 90000`, `simplify_mult2 = 1.5`, `simplify_max = 1000000`, all
read via `_p.get_uint(...)` under the comment `// These parameters are not
exposed` (`sat_config.cpp:149-153`). So round 1 lands at ~90 k conflicts, then
×1.5 spacing capped at +1 M.

**Order within one round** (`do_simplify`, `sat_solver.cpp:2064-2160`):
pop to base level → `m_cleaner` → `m_scc` (SCC of the binary implication graph
+ equivalent-literal substitution + transitive reduction) → `ext->pre_simplify`
→ `m_simplifier(false)` → `m_simplifier(true)` → `sort_watch_lits` →
`ext->simplify` → `m_probing` (failed literals) → `m_asymm_branch` → optional
lookahead simplify → threshold update → parallel clause exchange →
`anf_simplifier`.

Defaults (`src/sat/sat_simplifier_params.pyg`, `sat_params.pyg`):

| Pass | Default | Line |
|---|---|---|
| Bounded variable elimination (`elim_vars`, by resolution) | **on** | `:25` |
| Subsumption + self-subsuming resolution | **on** | `:31` |
| Asymmetric tautology elimination (`ate`), from round `bce_delay`=2 | **on** | `:7`, `:11` |
| SCC + transitive reduction (`scc`, `scc.tr`) | **on** | `sat_scc_params.pyg:4-5` |
| Probing, asymmetric branching (2 rounds, sampled) | **on** | `:25`, `sat_asymm_branch_params.pyg:4-9` |
| Blocked clause elim (`bce`), asymmetric BCE, covered clause elim (`cce`/`acce`) | off | `:4-8` |
| Blocked clause addition (`bca`) | off, and **forcibly disabled in code**: `m_bca = false && p.bca();` (`sat_simplifier.cpp:2113`) | `:10` |
| ANF/PDD simplification | off (`anf`, `anf.delay` 2) | `sat_params.pyg:78-80` |

BVE and BCE are additionally disabled in incremental mode, when assumptions are
being tracked, or multi-threaded (`bce_enabled_base`/`elim_vars_enabled`,
`sat_simplifier.cpp:101-115`), unless `sat.override_incremental` is set. This is
why `inc_sat_solver` must set the incremental flag *before* `updt_params`, with
an explanatory comment at `inc_sat_solver.cpp:103-110`.

**How proofs survive — mostly by not being emitted.** DRAT is enabled only when
`p.threads() == 1` (`sat_config.cpp:192-200`); with `threads > 1` proof logging
is **silently off**. Emission sites in the simplifier are exactly seven
(`sat_simplifier.cpp:130, 679, 682, 684, 1874, 1905, 2031`), and one of them —
BVE's deletion of binary clauses — is **commented out**
(`:1874`, `// m_drat.del(l, l2);`) while the long-clause path does delete
(`:1905-1907`). **BCE/ATE/CCE emit nothing at all**; neither does asymmetric
branching (`sat_asymm_branch.cpp` contains zero `m_drat` references) nor the ANF
simplifier. Instead, those passes record `sat_model_converter` entries so a model
can be repaired: BVE `s.m_mc.mk(model_converter::ELIM_VAR, v)` +
`save_clauses(...)` (`sat_simplifier.cpp:1995-1997`), BCE/CCE `m_mc.mk(k, l.var())`
(`:1591-1607`), ATE `m_mc.add_ate(...)` (`:1523, :1553`).

**And the internal DRAT checker is RUP-only.** `drat::verify` tries `is_drup`,
then `is_drat`, then `UNREACHABLE()` (`sat_drat.cpp:402-434`) — but
`drat::is_drat(unsigned n, literal const* c)` opens with an unconditional
`return false;` (`:354`), making the RAT logic below it dead code. The design is
internally consistent (nothing that needs RAT is logged), but it means
`sat.drat.check_unsat` checks DRUP, not DRAT.

**Contrast.** Our default run does **no CNF inprocessing at all**:
`cnf_inprocessing: false` (`backend.rs:390`), and `cnf_vivify: true` is a
documented no-op without it
([`11-wiring-and-integration.md`](../solver-inventory-2026-09/11-wiring-and-integration.md)
gap #1). We have five passes (`simplify`, `vivify`, `bve`, `compact`,
`xor_propagate`) against roughly twelve here, and ours are all config-gated off.
The proof story is inverted: our proof-carrying inprocessing module
(`axeyum-cnf/src/inprocess.rs`, ADR-1750) exists and has no caller, while the
shipping path hand-rolls three passes with a `ReductionLink` certificate; Z3's
inprocessing runs by default and simply does not log the passes that would need
RAT.

### E. Encoding / bit-blasting

- **Structural hashing** is a property of term construction, not a pass: the AST
  manager is fully hash-consed (`ast_manager::register_node_core`,
  `src/ast/ast.cpp:1657-1700`, which deallocates a duplicate and returns the
  existing pointer at `:1667-1677`). Every gate the bit-blaster emits is
  therefore shared automatically. The bit-blaster additionally memoizes
  per-declaration bit expansions in `obj_map<func_decl, expr*> m_const2bits`
  (`bit_blaster_rewriter.cpp:94`), with export/import for cross-context
  translation (`:767-796`) — which is what makes **incremental** lowering work
  (`inc_sat_solver.cpp:788` reuses an externally owned rewriter across calls).
- **Adder**: ripple-carry (`bit_blaster_tpl_def.h:139-157`) via `mk_carry`
  `:118`. No carry-lookahead, no parallel-prefix adder in the tree.
- **Multiplier**: a **carry-save array multiplier** — a grid of half- and
  full-adders — documented with an ASCII circuit diagram in the source
  (`bit_blaster_tpl_def.h:183-272`, diagram at `:212-232`). Not Booth, not a
  Wallace tree. A `mk_carry_save_adder` primitive exists (`:1110-1118`) but the
  multiplier does not build a Wallace/Dadda tree from it. Constants are folded
  first (`:187-198`), and `mk_const_case_multiplier` (`:1121-1181`)
  Shannon-expands each non-constant bit into an `ite` over two constant-folded
  products **when `2^(#non-constant bits)` beats the `5·sz²` circuit estimate**
  — the one place a genuinely different encoding is chosen.
- **Division**: restoring long division (`mk_udiv_urem` `:383-441`), signed
  variants by four-way sign case analysis (`:496-650`); power-of-two divisors
  become shifts (`:691-694`).
- **Shifts**: barrel shifter, log-many stages (`:872, :916, :959`).
- **CNF**: `src/sat/tactic/goal2sat.cpp` — Tseitin with root/polarity
  specialization (root-level formulas skip the definitional variable entirely,
  `:404-448`), a cache, and `ite_extra` **defaulting to true** (`:94`), which
  adds two redundant propagation-strengthening clauses per `ite` (`:527-530`).
  Every emitted clause carries a `tseitin` proof hint (`:104-136`). Interface
  variables are marked external so the simplifier will not eliminate them
  (`:318`, `collect_boolean_interface` `:929`).
- **AIG**: `src/tactic/aig/aig.cpp` (1,755 lines) with its own structural
  hashing. **It is in the default QF_BV pipeline** — `if_no_proofs(mk_aig_tactic())`
  nested under `when(memory < 300 MB)` (`qfbv_tactic.cpp:111-116`) — and reverts
  its own output when the round trip grew the term by more than 20 %
  (`aig_tactic.cpp:102-103`).
- **Cardinality/PB**: not CNF-encoded by default. `pb.solver = 'solver'`
  ("use native solver", `sat_params.pyg:60`) routes them to
  `src/sat/smt/pb_solver.cpp` as native constraints; `pb.resolve = cardinality`
  (`:63`). The sorting-network clausifier exists but is disabled — both

**Explanation is eager and push-based, not pull-based.** There is no
`theory::explain(literal)`. A theory must construct a self-contained
`smt::justification` **at propagation time**; `get_antecedents(conflict_resolution&)`
does not *return* literals, it **marks** them on the resolution object
(`smt_justification.h:68-74`), driving the loop at
`smt_conflict_resolution.cpp:192-214`. The concrete classes are
`theory_axiom_justification` (no antecedents, `:253`),
`theory_propagation_justification` (`:268`),
`ext_theory_propagation_justification` (literals **plus** `enode_pair`
equalities, `:337`), and their conflict duals. The canonical call site:

```cpp
ctx().assign(lit, ctx().mk_justification(
    ext_theory_propagation_justification(
        get_id(), ctx(), core.size(), core.data(),
        eqs.size(), eqs.data(), lit, ps.size(), ps.data())));
```
(`theory_lra.cpp:2626-2631`).

Our `TheorySolver::explain(ExplanationId) -> Option<Vec<TheoryLit>>`
(`euf_egraph.rs`, ADR-1701) is a *deferred* explanation handle, which the
legacy Z3 contract does not have. **The newer `src/sat/smt/` core does**:
theories propagate with an opaque `ext_justification_idx` and are asked later
via `get_antecedents(l, idx, r, probing)` (`sat_extension.h:93`). So on this one
point our design matches the newer of Z3's two cores, not the older. The cost is
recorded in Z3's own comment at `euf_solver.cpp:258-277`: theory-specific
justifications are *lost* under that scheme, and "for proof logging we use a
catch-all rule 'smt'".

**The combination algorithm is model-based, not eager Nelson-Oppen.**
`context::is_shared(enode*)` (`smt_context.cpp:4753-4826`):

- `num_th_vars >= 2` → shared (`:4824`);
- `m.is_ite(n)` → shared (`:4762`);
- exactly one theory var and some **parent** enode from a different family →
  shared (`:4780-4791`), unless `is_beta_redex`;
- otherwise ask the theory: `get_theory(th_id)->is_shared(l->get_var())`
  (`:4819`). A 25-line comment at `:4793-4818` explains why this hook is
  necessary: parametric theories (arrays of arrays, tuples) are *families* of
  theories that the core cannot separate by family id.

Interface equalities are then **proposed from the candidate model**:
`smt_theory.h:526 assume_eqs(VarValueTable&)` hashes each relevant-and-shared
theory variable by its current model value and calls `assume_eq` on each
collision. `context::assume_eq` (`smt_context.cpp:4695-4751`) internalizes the
equality atom, sets `set_eq_flag()` and `set_true_first_flag(v)` **before**
notifying theories (with the ordering rationale at `:4707-4713`), bumps
`m_stats.m_num_interface_eqs` (`:4729`), and forces relevance (`:4743`). So an
interface equality is a **branch with a preferred `true` phase**, created only
for pairs the theories' models say are equal. `theory_lra::assume_eqs`
(`:1593-1627`) calls `random_update()` first (`:1560-1591` →
`lp().random_update(...)` at `:1589`) so that variables which are merely
*coincidentally* equal separate before equalities are proposed.

**One theory does do eager Nelson-Oppen.** `theory_array_base::collect_shared_vars`
(`:547-578`) + `mk_interface_eqs()` (`:580-609`) internalizes and marks relevant
**all** pairwise equalities between same-sorted shared array roots, scheduled at
final check (`theory_array.cpp:459-461`) and interleaved with delayed axioms
(`:372-408`). So Z3 runs both disciplines side by side, chosen per theory.

**The round-robin.** `context::final_check` (`smt_context.cpp:4218-4311`):

1. quantifiers get a pre-pass, `m_qmanager->final_check_eh(false)` (`:4231`); a
   non-`FC_DONE` short-circuits;
2. then a loop over `range = num_theories + 1` slots (`:4241`), the extra slot
   being `m_qmanager->final_check_eh(true)` (`:4264`);
3. `m_final_check_idx` advances modulo `range` and **persists across calls**
   (`:4265`) — the rotation is fair over the whole search;
4. `FC_CONTINUE` returns immediately (`:4278`); `FC_GIVEUP` is recorded in
   `m_incomplete_theories` and the scan continues (`:4257-4261`);
5. when the index wraps, if any theory advertised `num_final_check_levels() > 1`
   the whole rotation re-runs at `level+1` **with the give-up state cleared**
   (`:4281-4293`) — the comment names `theory_lra`, whose level 2 runs the
   `nlsat` that level 1 skips.

Note also that `resolve_conflict` **resets all four propagation queues**
(`smt_context.cpp:4350-4353`), so a theory that buffers work in its own queue
must make it backtrack-aware — `theory_bv` documents a real bug from exactly this
(`theory_bv.cpp:1350-1366`).

**Against ours.** Our CDCL(T) drivers dispatch a *single* `TheorySolver`
(`cdclt.rs`, `native_cdclt.rs`); multi-theory cases are handled by purpose-built
fused solvers — `CombinedIncremental`, `CombinedIncrementalLia`,
`CombinedUfbvTheory` — that internally own two theories, rather than by a general
framework. There are 11 production `impl TheorySolver`. The four functions that
would have made this general have zero callers, with the search stated exactly
and a positive control (`classify_interface_equalities`, live at
`uflra_online.rs:70,613,916`) in
[`03-dispatch-routing-and-backends.md:387-400`](../solver-inventory-2026-09/03-dispatch-routing-and-backends.md).

### I. Model production

Assembly (`smt_model_generator.cpp:522-534`) is a six-phase pipeline:
`init_model` → `register_existing_model_values` → `mk_bool_model` → `mk_values`
→ `mk_func_interps` → `finalize_theory_models` → `register_macros`. For each
relevant e-node root, `mk_value_procs` (`:320-362`) asks the owning theory
`th->mk_value(r, *this)` (`:344`) or allocates a `fresh_value_proc` (`:351`);
`mk_values` then **topologically sorts** the value procs by dependency (`:307`)
so fresh values are materialized in a consistent order.

Function interpretations are built **partial by design** — the header says so:
"Create (partial) interpretation of function symbols. The 'else' is missing"
(`:420-423`) — one entry per congruence-class representative (`:438, :468`) —
and completed afterwards by `proto_model::complete_partial_func`
(`src/smt/proto_model/proto_model.cpp:345-357`), which picks a fresh value, else
the most frequent table result, else `get_some_value`. `func_interp` therefore
has an explicit else-branch that can legitimately be absent
(`is_partial()`, `src/model/func_interp.h:122`); `model.partial` (default false)
suppresses completion.

**Self-validation: present, off by default, and partial.**

- Enable switch is the *global* `model_validate` (default `false`,
  `src/params/context_params.cpp:164`) — there is no `smt.model_validate`.
- `smt_context.cpp:3632` then calls `th->validate_model(*m_proto_model)`. The
  base is `{}` (`smt_theory.h:387`); only **`theory_lra`** (`theory_lra.h:91`)
  and **`theory_seq`** (`theory_seq.h:426`) override it. Skipped entirely when
  recursive functions are present (`:3633-3634`). The stronger literal/clause
  check immediately below is `#if 0`'d (`:3639-3665`).
- `context::validate_model()` (`smt_context_inv.cpp:355-388`), the one actually
  called — from the MBQI loop at `smt_model_checker.cpp:477` — inspects only
  **relevant, ground** assigned literals and **returns `true` when evaluation
  fails**: `if (!m_proto_model->eval(n, res, false)) return true;` (`:372, :380`).
  Its failure mode is to pass.
- `cmd_context::validate_model()` (`cmd_context.cpp:2211-2267`) evaluates ground
  assertions only (`:2232`), skips quantified results (`:2246-2248`), and skips

  `update-field`, regexes, div/mod by zero, irrational algebraic numerals,
  **`as-array`, `store`, `map`, `const-array`** (`:2093-2131`), with the comment

  smt.core.validate. This option is for debugging only.")`

- No model is built at all when the last search failure was

### N. Incrementality

`combined_solver` (`src/solver/combined_solver.cpp`) is the API-level solver.
Its header comment (`:29-45`) states the design; the code matches:

- `assert_expr_core` `:160` / `assert_expr_core2` `:167` call `switch_inc_mode()`
  if `m_check_sat_executed`, then assert to **both** solvers;
- `push()` `:174` and `pop()` `:180` switch unconditionally;
- `get_consequences()` `:191` switches;
- `check_sat_core` `:210` switches when there are assumptions at all
  (`get_num_assumptions() != 0 || num_assumptions > 0 || m_ignore_solver1`) and
  delegates to solver2 (`:216-219`);
- once incremental, an `l_undef` from solver2 may *fall back* to solver1, subject
  to `combined_solver.solver2_unknown` (default 1 = "execute solver 1 if
  quantifier free") and an optional `solver2_timeout`.

So assumptions, unsat cores from assumptions, and `push`/`pop` all commit you to
`smt_context` and away from the tactic pipeline — **the largest and least
advertised performance cliff in Z3's incremental API, decided in four lines.**
Partial recovery exists: `simplifier_solver` (`src/solver/simplifier_solver.cpp`,
the `set-simplifier` feature) provides incremental preprocessing through
framework (3), whose `model_reconstruction_trail::replay` is precisely the
mechanism that makes preprocessing sound under interleaved
`push`/`assert`/`check`.

What survives a `pop`: the e-graph up to the scope, internalization, and the
phase cache. What does **not**: learned lemmas are deleted when popping below a
base level (`del_clauses(m_lemmas, bs.m_lemmas_lim)`,
`smt_context.cpp:2547`), and aux clauses are trimmed per scope (`:2560`). There
is no `smt.reset_lemmas` parameter in this tree; lemma hygiene is
`del_inactive_lemmas` (`:2842-2960`).

Unsat cores from assumptions: `init_assumptions` (`:3493-3534`) marks each
assumption's `bdata.m_assumption`; `mk_unsat_core` (`:3556-3591`) maps conflict
literals back through `m_literal2assumption`, **sorts by AST id for
determinism** (`:3582`), then optionally validates and lets a theory downgrade
the answer to `l_undef` (`:3586-3590`).

Ours: `Solver::push`/`pop` is a `Vec` watermark and `check` re-submits
everything (`solver.rs:126-142, :161-163`); `check_assuming` concatenates
assumptions onto assertions (`solver.rs:550-562`). Gap #4 in
[`11-wiring-and-integration.md`](../solver-inventory-2026-09/11-wiring-and-integration.md).
`IncrementalBvSolver` is the genuinely warm path and is a separate type.

### O. Parallelism

`parallel.enable` defaults **false** (`src/solver/parallel_params.pyg`) and is
described as enabling the parallel solver "by default on selected tactics (for
QF_BV)". Three independent engines:

1. **`src/sat/sat_parallel.cpp`** — clause sharing inside the CDCL SAT core:
   unit `exchange`, `share_clause` for binaries and longer, a shared learned-clause
   pool, gated by a size/quality filter `enable_add` (`:198`). Call sites in the
   solver at `sat_solver.cpp:457, :1662, :2690`.
2. **`src/smt/smt_parallel.cpp`** (2,023 lines) — cube-and-conquer for the SMT
   core over a shared search tree: unit/backbone sharing, conflict-core-driven
   cube pruning, dedicated backbone worker threads (`num_bb_threads` 2), an SLS
   portfolio thread, a core-minimizer thread, escalating per-thread conflict
   budgets. Carries acknowledged gaps in source: "we could be missing units"
   (`:970-975`).
3. **`src/solver/parallel_tactical.cpp`** (2,216 lines) — solver-level
   cube-and-conquer with node leasing and ancestor-path core-guided
   backtracking. It **refuses proofs** (one of the 20 `fail_if_proof_generation`
   callers).

Cube generation is `sat::solver::cube` (`sat_solver.cpp:1203`) or
`sat::lookahead::cube` (`sat_lookahead.cpp:2145`), selected by
`parallel.cube.lookahead` (default false → VSIDS activity, one split literal).

**Determinism under parallel: none, and nothing claims otherwise.** There is no
determinism flag and no reproducibility statement anywhere in the parallel code;
what there is instead is an explicit stale-worker race comment
(`smt_parallel.cpp:1428`, duplicated at `parallel_tactical.cpp:420`) and the
timing-dependent clause pool (`sat_parallel.cpp:204-210`). DRAT is also
**silently disabled** when `threads > 1` (`sat_config.cpp:192-200`).

Ours: `DEFAULT_INT_LINEAR_PORTFOLIO_WORKERS = 1` (`auto.rs:2948`), one hard-coded
two-arm group, threads only under `AXEYUM_PORTFOLIO_WORKERS >= 2`. Both projects
ship single-threaded by default.

### P. Resource limits and determinism

`src/util/rlimit.h` defines `reslimit` as a **deterministic counter**, not a
clock: `uint64_t m_count` `:35` against `uint64_t m_limit` `:36`, checked by
`inc()` `:73` and `is_active()` `:83`
(`m_suspend || (m_cancel == 0 && m_count <= m_limit && !is_timeout())`). Wall
clock is a separate, optional field (`m_timeout_ms` `:39`, and `is_timeout()`
compiles to `return false` unless `POLLING_TIMER` is defined, `:52`).
`scoped_rlimit` `:91-99` and `scoped_limits` `:126` push and pop nested budgets
with overflow saturation (`rlimit.cpp:50-66`); **child limits for threads and
sub-solvers roll their counts up into the parent** on pop
(`rlimit.cpp:77-100`), so a parallel run still spends one global budget.
`scoped_suspend_rlimit` (`:101-119`) protects regions that must not be
interrupted. Counter density: 48 `.inc()` sites across the tree.

Parameters: `timeout` (ms, default infinite) and `rlimit` (default 0 =
unrestricted) — `src/util/params.cpp:340-344`, parsed at
`src/params/context_params.cpp:66-70`; plus `solver.timeout`, `opt.timeout`,
`opt.rlimit`. Seeds: `smt.random_seed`, `sat.random_seed`, `sls.random_seed`,
`nlsat.seed`, all default 0.

**What is and is not guaranteed.** Same binary + same input + same params +
single thread + `rlimit` ⇒ reproducible; that is the design intent
(`RELEASE_NOTES.md:1047`, "New resource limit facilities to avoid
non-deterministic timeout behaviour"). Wall-clock `timeout` ⇒ not reproducible
by construction. Threads > 1 ⇒ not reproducible. Across versions ⇒ no documented
promise — I found no reproducibility statement in `RELEASE_NOTES.md`, `doc/`,
`README.md` or `Z3-AGENT.md`; note also `RELEASE_NOTES.md:78`, a fix for
"cross-compiler determinism" in rewriters, implying same-version results were
compiler-dependent until recently.

Our bench uses exactly this API: `z3_backend.rs:203` sets `random_seed` to a
constant `DETERMINISTIC_Z3_RANDOM_SEED`, `:206` sets `timeout`, `:212` sets
`rlimit` from `SolverConfig::resource_limit`.

**This is the one axis where Z3 is unambiguously ahead of the field**, and the
`reslimit` design — hierarchical, thread-aware, parent roll-up, RAII scoping —
is worth copying rather than reinventing.

### What replacing Z3 would actually require

Given the above, the capability we would need to reproduce is: **a second,
independent, complete decision procedure for the fragments the fuzzes generate,
answering sat/unsat/unknown under a timeout.** Concretely:

| Fragment | Generated by | What an alternative oracle must decide |
|---|---|---|
| QF_LRA with Boolean structure, plus `/` by a constant, a nonzero constant, and a variable | `qf_lra_differential_fuzz.rs` | Linear real arithmetic; `/`-by-zero as an underspecified but *congruent* value (the suite pins this in five dedicated tests, `:459, :490, :516, :553, :588`) |
| Dense conjunctive QF_LRA, 10–14 vars | `simplex_lra_fallback_differential.rs` | Same, at a size that defeats Fourier–Motzkin |
| QF_UFLRA, one unary function | `qf_uflra_differential_fuzz.rs` | LRA + congruence |

Nothing about models, cores, proofs, tactics or incrementality is on that list.
That is a much smaller replacement target than "Z3", and it is one that other
reference solvers in this folder (OpenSMT, Yices2, cvc5) already meet. **The
blocker on ADR-0002's demotion is not the oracle's capability; it is that we have
no second independent verdict source wired in at all.** A concrete first step
that costs nothing in soundness: point one of the three suites at a second
solver and require agreement from both.

## Not comparable

- **"Preprocessing pipeline" does not name one thing on either side.** Z3 has
  three frameworks whose passes overlap by name, plus pairs of live
  implementations under different names (`elim-uncnstr`/`elim-uncnstr2`,
  `propagate-values`/`propagate-values2`, `reduce-args`/`reduce-args2`); we have
  two pipelines whose passes overlap by name and neither of which is a superset
  of the other. Any head-to-head "how many passes" number is arithmetic on
  different denominators. The comparison that *is* sound is the one in Axis B:
  which ordered sequence a **default** call executes — nine slots for Z3's
  `simplifier_solver`, five for our `auto::preprocess_reduce`, and **zero
  tactic-level passes for QF_LRA specifically**.
- **Tactic count vs route count.** Z3's 118 tactics and our 51 dispatcher route
  labels are not the same kind of object. A tactic is a goal→goals transformer
  the user can name and compose; a route label is a trace string recording which
  internal branch fired. Comparing 118 to 51 would be a category error.
- **Proof "support".** Z3 has had a proof API for fifteen years and produces
  something for almost every route. We produce nothing on eight `unsat` routes
  (`Evidence::Unsat(None)`) and something independently checkable on the rest. A
  row that says "both produce proofs" is worse than useless. The honest axis is
  *what a third party can re-derive from the artifact without trusting the
  producer*, and on that axis Z3's bit-vector, array, datatype, quantifier and
  preprocessing steps are trust assertions.
- **Z3's `smt_context` and `src/sat/smt/euf_solver` are two solvers in one
  repository**, selected by `sat.euf`/`sat.smt` and by logic. Many statements are
  true of one and false of the other — including which proof format exists, which
  theories exist (the new core has **no sequence theory**), and whether
  explanation is push- or pull-based. Every claim above says which core it is
  about; a summary that drops that qualification will be wrong about half the
  time.
- **The oracle relationship makes some comparisons circular.** Our differential
  fuzzes define "correct" as "what Z3 says". A gap analysis cannot then also use
  those fuzzes as evidence that we match Z3 on correctness — they are evidence
  that we have not yet found a disagreement, on the shapes the generators emit,
  at the sizes they emit. That is a real but bounded claim.
- **`src/muz/` (75,654 lines) has no counterpart on our side.** Horn clauses and
  Datalog are a different product, not a feature we are behind on. Including them
  would inflate the gap in a way that says nothing about the SMT comparison.
- **Age asymmetry cuts both ways.** Some of what reads as Z3 weakness is
  accumulated history a fifteen-year-old codebase carries and a two-year-old one
  does not: superposition-prover code under `#if 0`, a README naming a deleted
  directory, an example calling a removed parameter, a `core_maxsat` engine name
  with no branch. None of those affect a verdict. They are cited above because
  they bear on *maintenance of the proof and checking machinery specifically*,
  which is the axis we are competing on — not as general quality signals.
