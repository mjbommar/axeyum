**Item 3 was free, and nobody had measured it.** +0.6 s. Both reverts were

**The route the brief asked for works, and the first construction of it was
already correct.** `797100410` — collapse each term to a flat literal by an
interleaved walk, then close the sum by a bound rather than an evaluation —
was **accepted by the kernel**, which neither reverted attempt ever achieved,
at 587 s. It was not wrong; it was 5.8× too slow. The fix was to move where
the bound is taken, not to change the method.

**A cheap sign lemma for `negative ≤ 0` does exist and is not what this
needed.** `Rat.neg_nonpos_of_nonneg : 0 ≤ a → neg a ≤ 0` (`rat_prelude/
group.rs:547`) and the numerator bridge `Rat.nonneg_of_int_nonneg` /
`Rat.int_nonneg_of_nonneg` (`rat_prelude/laws.rs:162`) are both there. They
close `q ≤ 0` once you already hold `q`. The cost was never the final `≤`; it
was *forming* `q = -13/1875` from the three series terms. So the answer to the
brief's question is: yes, and it does not help.

**What actually costs, and it is neither attribution offered before.**
`NatOps::num` builds a **unary** numeral, `succ^n Nat.zero`. The kernel's
binary `Nat` acceleration (`tc.rs::reduce_nat_binop`, covering add, sub, mul,
div, mod, gcd, pow, beq, ble) requires each argument to whnf to a `Lit::Nat`,
and `reduce_nat_succ` converts only `succ (Lit k)` — never
`succ (Const Nat.zero)`. A unary numeral therefore **never becomes a literal,
so nothing in this prelude accelerates**: every `Nat.gcd` and every `Nat.div`
inside `Rat.normalize` runs by recursion. Cost is superlinear in the magnitudes
reached, which makes *the largest `Nat` ever formed* the quantity to minimise.
"Operand magnitude" was half right and "nesting depth" was a proxy; the
governing fact is magnitude **with no acceleration**, and it is a property of
the whole `Nat`/`Int`/`Rat` prelude, not of this file.

**The three magnitude reductions, in the order they matter:**

1. **Bound two factors of `R` EARLY** (`cos_wide_term_bounded`). `8/75 ≤ 7/64`
   cross-multiplies to **525**; reaching the same conclusion through the exact
   `512/1875 ≤ 7/25` costs **13,125**. The two remaining factors ride
   `CReal.mul_le_mul_of_nonneg_left` (conjugated by `mul_comm` — there is no
   `_right` form in `CRealPrelude`) instead of an evaluation, landing on
   `7/64 → 7/40 → 7/25` at 320 and 200. `7/64` is *forced*, not chosen: it is
   `(7/25) / (8/5)²`, the unique value that lands exactly on the bound the sum
   needs. **This single change is 587 s → 113 s.**
2. **Evaluate one factor at a time** (`cos_wide_term_small`):
   `1/24 → 1/15 → 8/75 → 64/375 → 512/1875` instead of forming
   `Rat.pow (8/5) 4 = 4096/625` and then `Rat.mul (1/24) (4096/625)`, whose
   normalize is `normalize 4096 15000`. One `CReal.mul_assoc` per factor plus
   one `CReal.mul_one` at the base buys the interleaved order; `pow R (2j)` is
   defeq to the left-nested product by ι, so exposing the factors is free.
3. **Close the sum by `add_le_add`**, never by evaluating
   `Rat.add (Rat.add 1 (-32/25)) (512/1875)`. That addition cross-multiplies to
   `25 * 1875 = 46,875` and then needs `Nat.gcd 325 46875` plus two `Nat.div`s
   at that size — precisely what both reverted attempts were paying for. The
   value `-13/1875` is **never built** anywhere in this file.

Largest `Nat` formed anywhere in the numeric leaf is now **625**, in
`Rat.add (-7/25) (7/25) = 0/1`.

**What the kernel rejected: nothing.** Both declarations were accepted on the
first `add_declaration` attempt in every variant that compiled. The failures
this lane hit were all *time*, never a `TypeMismatch`, and the two prior briefs'
kernel-facts list (`le_congr` direction, `Rat`'s `reduced` field, `neg (neg x)`
not defeq, `Nat.add` recursing right) was accurate and is why.

**One measurement did not run:** an attempted bisect inside item 4 — declaring
only the numeric leaf, without `alternating_upper_bound_tail` — was killed at
620 s and reported nothing. It is superseded: with the leaf now costing ~12 s
total, the split it was chasing no longer matters. Note the trap it exposed,
which cost this lane a run: `scripts/cargo-serialized.sh` takes a host-wide
flock, so a `cargo test` timing under lane contention measures the **queue**,
not the kernel. Both long readings above come from running
`target/debug/deps/axeyum_lean_kernel-*` directly, which takes no lock at all.

**For whoever takes rung 3.** The unary-numeral finding generalises past this
file: any future `Rat` leaf should be sized by "largest `Nat` formed", and a
bound placed one multiplication earlier is worth more than any restructuring of
the same computation. If someone wants the general fix rather than the local
one, it is to make `reduce_nat_succ` (or a `NatOps::num` that emits
`Lit::Nat`) bridge unary numerals to literals — that would switch the
acceleration on for the whole prelude, and is a `tc.rs`/`nat_prelude` change,
out of this lane's scope.
