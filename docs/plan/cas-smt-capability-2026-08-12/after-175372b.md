## The route does arithmetic, not pattern-matching (axis U)

Axis U was written *after* seeing the new route, specifically to try to make it
answer wrongly. All ten entries have the syntactic shape `cas-int-units` fires
on — a product of variables equal to a small constant under a lower bound — but
six are **satisfiable**:

| query | expect | after | which route fired |
|---|---|---|---|
| `U2` `a >= 2 /\ a*p = a` | sat | `sat` | `nia-linearize` |
| `U3` `a >= 2 /\ a*p = 0` | sat | `sat` | `nia-linearize` |
| `U4` `a*p = 1` (no bound) | sat | `sat` | `nia-linearize` |
| `U6` `a,b >= 2 /\ a*b = 4` | sat | `sat` | `nia-linearize` |
| `U8` `a >= 2 /\ a*p = 2` | sat | `sat` | `nia-linearize` |
| `U10` `a,p,r >= 2 /\ a*p*r = 8` | sat | `sat` | `nia-linearize` |

**`cas-int-units` fired on zero satisfiable queries in the whole 103-query
corpus.** It declines on `a*p = 0`, on `a*p = 2`, on `a*p = a`, and on the
unbounded `a*p = 1` — each of which a shape-matching implementation would have
refuted. That is the strongest single piece of evidence that the new route is
sound rather than lucky.

The axis F traps are the ones a zero-test-based route is most likely to get
wrong: `x·x = 2` is `unsat` over ℤ while `x² − 2` is not the zero polynomial.
Nothing in the change turned any of them into a wrong `sat`, and no `cas-*`
route claimed any of them.

The four tripwires deserve emphasis: `x² − 109y² = 1 ∧ y ≥ 1` is satisfiable,
but its smallest witness is `y = 15140424455100`. Returning `unsat` there would
be a wrong answer produced by a bounded abstraction. Both commits return
`unknown`. That is the correct conservative behaviour and it survived the change.

So the change closes the **unit** shape (`a·p = ±1`) but not the **variable
divisor** shape (`a | x` for a non-constant `a`, `A9`/`A12`), not the opaque
window lemma (`B1`/`B5`), and not the monolithic-versus-decomposed axis
(`C1`/`C7`/`C11`/`C13`) that route B identified as its main obstacle. Three of
these decline in under a fifth of a second regardless of budget and survive a 6×
budget increase, so they remain structural.

## Verdict

**A genuine capability gain, correctly implemented, but a small one — and it is
not the gain the identity route was built for.**

- Genuine: 5 queries move from a 10 s timeout to `unsat` in 0.00 s, on ground
  truth established independently of axeyum, and the one that matters most
  (`A1`) is the exact shape a prior session recorded as the tool's sharpest
  limit.
- Correctly implemented: `cas-int-units` declined on all **54** satisfiable
  queries, including six deliberately built to look exactly like the shape it
  refutes. No `cas-*` route decided a single `sat`-expected query.
- Small: 5 of 111. `cas-identity-refuter` added **zero** new decisions — axis D
  was already saturated at the baseline, so the polynomial-identity work is a
  re-attribution on this corpus, and any claim for it must rest on certificate
  quality rather than on capability.
- Not an artifact of how the queries were posed: `A1`/`A3`/`U7`/`U9`/`N7` are
  three or four lines each, carry a `sat` control differing by one constant, and
  were written before the route existed (`A1`/`A3`) or specifically to break it
  (`U7`/`U9`/`N7`).
