## What

Re-run `check_auto_explained` over the LCG corpus builders in `tests/route_trace.rs` plus the
committed corpus, and check the catalogue against observed dispatch: every attempted label is known;
attempt order is a *subsequence* of `(phase, order_index)`; every `Decided` verdict is permitted by
the edge's `verdicts`; attempted routes' `requires`/`forbids` held.

## Files touched

- new `crates/axeyum-solver/tests/bridge_catalogue_replay.rs`
- `crates/axeyum-solver/src/auto.rs` (test-visible `Features` accessor; `depth` marker in the
  `dispatch_reduced` re-entry at `auto.rs:1566`) -- additive only
- fixture corpus

## Exit criteria

- Subsequence / guard / verdict checks green over LCG and committed corpora
- A mutation fixture (perturb one `order_index` or `forbids` bit) makes the validator fail
- Edge-coverage percentage reported
- Verdict-invariance and `progress_frontier` ratchets green (this touches `auto.rs`)

## First coverage baseline, measured 2026-08-01

T0.1's `--json` mode makes the edge-coverage metric this task must report
computable *today*, before the validator exists. Over `corpus/regression` at a
3 s budget, 127 decided files produced traces containing **22 distinct route
labels**.

`auto.rs` carries roughly **45** labels, so the committed regression corpus
exercises only about **half** the catalogue. Observed decline reasons split
`incomplete` 16 · `unsupported` 12 · `not-applicable` 9 · `budget` 3.

Two consequences for this task:

1. **Coverage reporting is not optional.** A validator that passes while
   silently never touching half the edges reads as "the catalogue is correct"
   when it is only "the exercised half is consistent." Report the percentage.
2. **The corpus needs widening before the validator means much.** Pair this with
   T2.5 (public-corpus supplement per atlas row); the regression corpus alone
   cannot exercise the quantifier, string, or FP routes.

Reproduce with:

```sh
cargo run -q -p axeyum-bench --example explain_corpus -- corpus/regression 3000 --json
```

## Notes and traps

**Subsequence semantics, not equality.** Silent `Ok(None)` guard skips (e.g. `auto.rs:2492`) mean
unattempted routes legitimately vanish from a trace.

Without the depth marker, reduced-query attempts are flattened into the outer trace and will be
misattributed to the original query's guard check, producing spurious failures.

An over-strong `forbids` stays invisible under subsequence semantics -- mitigate with per-route
fixture pins in the style of `tests/route_trace.rs:299-504` and honest coverage reporting.
