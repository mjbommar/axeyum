# ADR-0746: the safety matrix is generated and gated, and it says the generated facts are the best-protected rows

Status: accepted
Date: 2026-08-30
Index-summary: S0 of ADR-0717 measured. 2,117 proved facts x 9 protections,
generated by `scripts/gen-safety-matrix.py` and gated in both aggregates.
Median protections per fact is 3; the thin spots are statement pinning
(142/2117), per-theorem footprint (59), semantic falsification (91), mutation
controls (14) and independent Lean replay (8). Largest checker fan-out is 463.
**1,901 evidence rows declare a semantic `kind` while recording an axiom
footprint**, so reading `kind` at face value would over-report semantic
falsification twentyfold. The `generated-unreviewed` rows are the
BEST-protected part of the ledger, not the worst — and the thing none of them
protects is `formal.statement`.
Lane: `l0-safety-matrix`

Implements: [ADR-0717](adr-0717-library-construction-is-graph-directed-through-an-artifact-compatible-trust-anchor.md) phase S0,

## Context

ADR-0717's threat model names five risks kernel acceptance alone does not
cover — kernel unsoundness, statement error, vacuity, contamination, false
evidence — and states that an empty `axiom_footprint` addresses only part of
two of them. S0's job is to say, with a denominator, which protections each
proved ledger row actually carries, so that S1–S6 are designed against a
measurement rather than an impression.

Four of the five risks produced live instances in the session that preceded
this one: two `ml430` mirrors whose upstream statement was overwritten with our
own `render_lean` output (statement error); two negative controls that could
not fail (vacuity); two held-out families that were never blind (contamination);
and a planted bad-writer control that was vacuous against any subject but its
own (false evidence).

## Decision

`scripts/gen-safety-matrix.py` generates `artifacts/safety-matrix/` — one TSV
row per proved fact with nine protection columns plus checker fan-out, and a
summary. `--check` is registered in `scripts/check.sh` and the justfile and
refuses on drift or on a control failure.

Three rules the generator holds to, each of which changed a number:

1. **Nothing is inferred from a neighbouring fact.** Every cell is read from
   the fact file or from a ledger-wide manifest that names the fact by id.
2. **The subject is taken only from an explicit binding.** The ledger's own
   `theorem_of` fallback — the first dotted name in a checker command — is
   reproduced as a separate, weaker column, because its own docstring calls it
   "demonstrably NOT reliable in general".
3. **Evidence `kind` is not read at face value.** See below.

## What the census found

2,270 facts, 2,117 `proved`. Coverage:

| protection | facts | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| `env_footprint` | 1859 | 87.8% |
| `kernel_theorem` (explicit binding) | 1466 | 69.2% |
| `coverage_bearing_checker` | 1442 | 68.1% |
| `exact_statement` (drift pin) | 142 | 6.7% |
| `semantic_falsification` | 91 | 4.3% |
| `per_theorem_footprint` | 59 | 2.8% |
| `circularity` | 38 | 1.8% |
| `mutation_control` | 14 | 0.7% |
| `independent_replay` | 8 | 0.4% |

Median protections per proved fact: 3. 53 facts hold none of the nine; 523 hold
exactly one, and for 400 of those the one is the prelude-wide axiom sweep.

### The `kind` enum has lost its discriminating power

**1,901 evidence rows carry `kind: exhaustive-enumeration` or `instance-pin`
while their `supports` reads `axiom_footprint: [] -- ...`.** Nothing was
enumerated and no instance was pinned; the row records that
`Kernel::axiom_footprint` came back empty.

A census reading `kind` at face value would have credited those 1,901 rows with
semantic falsification, turning a true 91 into 1,992 — the single largest
over-count available in this data, and it would have pointed S3 at a problem
that was already solved. This is exactly the failure the fact schema's own note
records about `check_status: checked`, arriving in a second field. The
classifier therefore reads `supports`, and two controls pin both polarities.

### The statement-drift gate covers 6.8% of settled facts and passes

`scripts/check-settled-fact-statements.py` prints
`settled=2119|pinned=144|drifted=0` and **exits 0**. A settled fact absent from
the pin manifest is read as "newly settled", not as a violation, so 1,975 proved
facts have no protection against their `formal.statement` being edited to match
whatever was proved. The gate is not wrong — drift is a property of two
snapshots and it correctly reports zero drift among the rows it holds — but its
denominator is not the ledger, and nothing said so before this census.

### Checker fan-out: 2,284 commands, largest 463

The largest fan-out is **463** facts on
`nat_axiom_inventory --include-constructed --require-axiom-free creal`, then 318
and 280 on the two `nat` sweeps. Seven commands each cover more than 100 facts.
2,221 of the 2,284 commands are used by exactly one fact.

So the ledger is not mostly shared evidence: only **48** proved facts have no
checker of their own, and 17 cite no checker at all. But a typical generated row
carries exactly two checkers, and one of them is a prelude-wide sweep shared
with hundreds of siblings. Describing such a row as carrying "two independent
checks" is wrong. The honest description is one fact-specific check plus a share
in one prelude-wide check.

### The `generated-unreviewed` rows are the best-protected part of the ledger

This is the finding that most contradicts the prior expectation.

| curation | facts | median protections | own-subject checker | statement pin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `generated-unreviewed` | 1038 | 3 | 1038 / 1038 | 0 |
| `curated` | 12 | 3 | 12 / 12 | 0 |
| (unset) | 1067 | **1** | **392 / 1067** | 142 |

Every one of the 1,038 generated rows carries a discriminating checker naming
its own declaration; only 37% of the hand-authored rows do. The generator
emitted a uniform template — `theorem_dependency_inventory -- <Name> | grep -cE
'^<Name>[[:space:]]'` plus the prelude sweep — and uniformity is exactly what a
census rewards.

**What those rows are protected by, stated precisely.** Per fact: the named
declaration exists in the kernel environment as a `Declaration::Theorem`, with
the exit status depending on that finding through two independent failure modes.
Shared with up to 462 siblings: the whole prelude admits no `Axiom`, `Opaque` or
`Quotient`.

**What they are not protected by, and this is the gap that matters.** Nothing
re-derives `formal.statement`. The fact's own prose says "the authoritative
content of this fact is `formal.statement`" and no checker in the ledger reads
it. The per-fact command matches the declaration NAME and never its TYPE, and
none of the 1,038 is in the drift-pin manifest. A prior audit sampling 40 found
39 of 40 statements verbatim from the kernel render; that is reassuring about
how they were produced and is not a protection, because it is not re-run.

So the honest description of the 1,035–1,038 generated rows is: **their proof
terms are kernel-checked, their declarations are existence-checked per fact,
their prelude is footprint-checked in a batch, and their statements are checked
by nothing at all.** The unreviewed part is the prose, as expected — but the
unprotected part is the formal statement, which was not.

## What a gate over this matrix refuses

Measured, not asserted. Six mutations, three distinct guards:

| mutation | refused by |
|---|---|
| delete a fact named by a control | `CONTROL` (2 fire) |
| drop a `checker_command` from that fact | `CONTROL` |
| silently unsettle a proved fact | `CONTROL` |
| delete an **uncontrolled** proved fact | `DRIFT` |
| swap an own-subject checker for the shared prelude sweep | `DRIFT` |
| break a protection classifier so it matches nothing | `CONTROL` |

The fifth is the one that justifies the gate. Downgrading a fact's own checker
to the `--require-axiom-free creal` sweep it already shares with 462 siblings is
invisible to every other gate: the fact still cites a discriminating command,
`validate-facts.py` is satisfied, and nothing else counts how many facts lean on
one run.

The sixth is the one that keeps the census honest about itself. A classifier
that stops matching reports a cheerful zero for a whole column, which reads as a
finding rather than a broken instrument — this repository's most reliable way of
manufacturing a false measurement.

## Consequences

- S1 (statement identity) is the largest gap by count: 1,975 proved facts with
  no statement binding of any kind, and the `generated-unreviewed` population is
  the whole of the machine-produced ledger.
- S3 (semantic falsification) starts from 91, not from the 1,992 the `kind`
  field would suggest.
- S4 (independent replay) starts from 8.
- A `just check-theorem` front door must treat "cites a discriminating command"
  and "cites a command whose subject is this fact" as different rows in its
  table. 68.1% of the ledger has the second; the ledger's regex fallback would
  claim 82.4% and would be guessing for 302 of them.
- No fact was edited by this lane. Where a row overstates itself the repair
  belongs to S1.

## Alternatives considered

**Hand-author the matrix as a document.** Rejected: ADR-0717's exit criterion is
that deleting a fact or checker row fails the census, which a document cannot
do, and this repository's standing rule is that a checker which cannot fail is
worse than no checker.

**Credit a fact with the protections its family carries.** Rejected explicitly.
That is the inference that produced two of the four live risk instances this
phase was calibrated against.

**Trust `theorem_of`'s regex fallback for subject binding.** Rejected: it would
raise `coverage_bearing_checker` from 68.1% to 82.4% by guessing, and the
guessing is documented to have been wrong at least twice in this ledger.
